# RV32IM + Branch Prediction — Track A Modifications to the Development Plan

Companion to `RISC-V_NTT.txt`, which it never edits. Same target platform, same
budget: 63,400 LUTs, 126,800 FFs, 240 DSP48E1, 135 × 36 Kb BRAM, 100 MHz
oscillator. Track A as originally written is finished — A0 through A13, M0
through M7, tagged `m7-fmax-and-benchmarks`. This document adds A14 through A19
and milestones M7.1 and M7.2, and exists because two of A13's measured numbers
say the original scope was one extension short of the baseline the original
document itself assumes.

---

## 0. How to read this against RISC-V_NTT.txt

**`RISC-V_NTT.txt` is never modified.** It is the record of what was planned and
built, and the `m7-fmax-and-benchmarks` tag is the record of what that produced.
Everything here is additive.

Three markers are used, and they mean different things:

- **OVERRIDES §x** — the original text is now wrong and this replaces it. There
  are two of these today, both listed in §2; A17 may add a third, and if it does
  it goes in that table rather than in a commit message.
- **EXTENDS §x** — the original stands and something is added on top. The
  original's "done when" remains satisfied by what was already built.
- **CLARIFIES §x** — the original was ambiguous or rested on an unstated
  assumption. Nothing changes; the assumption is written down.

**A numbering hazard, stated once.** `RISC-V_NTT.txt` uses `M1`–`M5` in §10 as
*measurement* headings and `M0`–`M16` in §12 as *milestones*. They are different
sequences with colliding names. `M7.1` and `M7.2` here are **milestones**, in
§12's sequence. Nothing in this document adds to §10's sequence; where §10's
measurements are amended, they are named as "§10 M2", never bare.

## 1. Why this exists

Two measurements, both taken after A13 was complete and tagged.

**The first.** The reference ML-KEM NTT, identical source, identical `-O2`,
dynamic instruction count on Spike:

| ISA | instructions per `ntt()` |
|---|---|
| `rv32i_zicsr` | 148,645 |
| `rv32im_zicsr` | 23,795 |

**6.25×.** Now read §B6 of the original: *"a software NTT on your RV32I core of
roughly 15,000–30,000 cycles depending on compiler quality."* The RV32IM number
lands inside that band. The RV32I number is 7–14× outside it. **§B6's baseline
estimate was written assuming a hardware multiplier**, while §1.5 scopes the core
without one. That contradiction is in the original document, not in the
implementation, and it is not a cosmetic one: at §B6's P=4 (~224 cycles) the
headline kernel speedup is ~150× against an RV32IM baseline and ~900× against
ours.

It is worse than a scaling factor, because it is not uniform. ML-KEM's
polynomial arithmetic is multiply-bound and Keccak has no multiplies at all, so
on an RV32I machine the NTT slice of the profile is inflated and the Keccak slice
is not. §10 M3 asks for exactly that breakdown and warns that presenting it
honestly matters more than a big number. **On RV32I hardware that breakdown is
distorted no matter how it is reported**, which is why this is a silicon change
and not a wording change.

**The second.** Stall attribution on the real RTL over both A13 benchmarks,
counting `stall` cycles and `ex_redirect` pulses (each redirect flushes two
younger slots). The identity `cycles = retired + stalls + 2 × redirects` closes
with **residual zero**, and the region totals reproduce the hardware numbers to
the cycle:

| | Dhrystone | CoreMark |
|---|---|---|
| cycles | 1,558,048 | 1,040,943 |
| retired | 1,126,032 (IPC 0.7227) | 721,374 (IPC 0.6930) |
| **branch/jump flushes** | **380,016 — 24.4%** | **298,856 — 28.7%** |
| load-use interlocks | 52,000 — 3.3% | 20,713 — 2.0% |
| taken branches | 120,000 | 122,930 |
| not-taken branches | 74,001 | 75,171 |
| JAL | 38,004 | 14,962 |
| JALR | 32,004 | 11,536 |

Control flow is ~90% of the lost cycles. The forwarding network is doing its
job; the fetch is not. That is what makes A13's *optional improvement loop* worth
taking, and it is why it is specified here rather than left optional.

## 3. Conflict analysis

The instruction was to look hard for things that become intractable later. Four
candidates turned out not to be problems, two are genuinely hard, and one is a
budget to watch.

### 3.1 What is *not* a conflict, and why — each of these was checked

**Opcode space.** The obvious catastrophe would be `Xkntt` overlapping `M`.
It does not. `docs/isa-spec.md` §2 puts Tier 1 in `custom-0` (`0b0001011`,
`0x0B`) and Tier 2 in `custom-1` (`0b0101011`, `0x2B`); `M` lives in `OP`
(`0x33`) with `funct7 = 0000001`. Disjoint, with no shared `funct3`/`funct7`
decode. **The strict-reserved-field rule survives intact**: `funct7` values other
than `0000000`, `0100000` and now `0000001` remain illegal in `OP`, and nothing
in `custom-0`/`custom-1` changes at all.

**Toolchain.** Verified, not assumed: `riscv-none-elf-gcc --print-multi-lib`
lists `rv32im/ilp32`, and `-march=rv32im_zicsr -mabi=ilp32
-print-libgcc-file-name` resolves to a real `rv32im/ilp32/libgcc.a`. No toolchain
work, no new multilib, no rebuild.

**Spike.** `M` is native. **No fork change**, so `toolchain/patches.sh export
spike` is unaffected and `patches_in_sync` stays green. Only the `--isa` strings
in `tb/cosim/*` change.

**DSP budget.** 240 DSP48E1 available. `M`'s 32×32→64 multiplier takes at most 4.
§B1 specifies 3 per Montgomery multiplier plus 2 for Barrett; §B6 estimates
14–20 at P=4 and the arithmetic scales to roughly 50–60 at P=16. Worst case
~65 of 240. There is no contention, and there was never going to be.

**§B6's done-when gets *easier*.** It requires "the coprocessor's standalone
Fmax exceeds the core's Fmax". Adding `M` and a predictor lowers the core's
Fmax. This is the one place where the cost here is a benefit elsewhere, and it
should be read as a warning that the criterion is weak rather than as good news.

### 3.2 Genuinely hard, first: riscv-formal depth versus divider latency

`tb/formal/run_riscv_formal.py` documents why the BMC depth is 14 and not more:
it is derived from the deepest property the check set has to reach — a register
dependency at distance 3, plus a load-use stall, plus a two-cycle control bubble
— and *"BMC time here blows up superlinearly with depth: `formal_regfile` proves
at depth 8 in about three seconds and was still grinding at step 13 of depth 20
after eight minutes."*

**A multi-cycle instruction extends every one of those windows by its latency
minus one.** A 3-cycle `MUL` needs roughly depth 17, which is cheap. A 32-cycle
iterative `DIV` needs roughly depth 46, which is very likely not to converge in
any reasonable time.

Three ways out, in order of preference. Take the first that works; do not skip to
the third because the first was inconvenient.

1. **Per-check depth.** Keep the existing 43 checks at 14, run `insn_mul*` at
   the depth `MUL`'s latency requires, and give `insn_div*`/`insn_rem*` whatever
   converges. Verify that riscv-formal's `genchecks.py` honours per-check depth
   entries before designing around it.
2. **Prove the divider standalone.** This project already proves `rvntt_alu`,
   `rvntt_regfile`, `rvntt_branch`, `rvntt_hazard` and `rvntt_csr` as isolated
   modules. Prove the divider the same way — against a reference division,
   including the two mandated edge cases — at whatever depth its own latency
   needs, with no pipeline around it. riscv-formal then only has to establish
   that the *pipeline* retires it correctly, which is a shallower property.
3. **Record the boundary.** M6 already states, precisely, that it does not cover
   `dmem`, the `bus_*` family, CSRs, or the `Xkntt` encodings. A recorded gap is
   worth more than an unrun check, and far more than a check that was made to
   pass by weakening it.

**What you must not do is lower the global depth to make `DIV` fit.** That
weakens 43 existing checks to accommodate 8 new ones, and it would not be visible
in any report.

### 3.3 Genuinely hard, second: the independent cycle model

`tb/cosim/cycle_model.py` predicts

    span = (retired − 1) + stalls + flush_penalties

and its value is stated in its own header: a phantom stall changes no
architectural state, so a commit-log diff cannot see it. Its independence comes
from decoding the *retired instruction stream* with `model/rv32i_ref.py` rather
than from the RTL's own hazard predicate.

**Both A14 and A19 break it, in different ways, and the second is the awkward
one.**

- **A14** adds `Σ(latency − 1)` for each multi-cycle `M` instruction. This is
  easy: the latency is a property of the opcode, the model already decodes the
  opcode, and the term is additive. Independence is preserved.
- **A19** changes `flush_penalties` from `2 × redirects` to `2 × mispredicts`,
  and **a mispredict count cannot be derived from the retired stream alone.**

The wrong fix is to read the RTL's predictor state, which would destroy exactly
the independence the model exists for. **The right fix is to model the predictor
from its specification** — a 2-bit bimodal with a defined index function, a
direct-mapped tagged BTB with a defined replacement policy, an 8-entry RAS with
defined push/pop rules — and let the model simulate that spec against the
dynamic branch stream. That stays independent of the implementation while
predicting its timing, and it has a valuable side effect: **it forces the
predictor to have a written specification precise enough to be simulated**,
which is the difference between a predictor you can verify and one you can only
benchmark.

This makes the predictor's specification (A19) a deliverable in its own right,
not a description of whatever the RTL happens to do.

### 3.4 A budget, not a conflict: Fmax headroom

A14 and A19 both add logic to a design with 0.170 ns of slack. **The reason it
has so little is not what A12 concluded**, and the correction is load-bearing
enough to restate here: reading the post-route path hop by hop shows the
core-to-BRAM crossing is under a fifth of it, and about half is a logical
dependency chain — forwarding mux, then the full ALU result mux, then the
misaligned-address check, then the trap gating the byte enables. `rtl/soc/CLAUDE.md`
carries the breakdown. **A17 exists because of that finding**, and it is
deliberately sequenced before the predictor so the one change here with real
Fmax risk lands on a design that has margin to spend.

**Set the floor explicitly.** §A12 already says *"if you're under 60 [MHz], look
at the forwarding mux depth and whether your branch target adder shares a path
with the comparator."* Adopt that as a stop condition: if the core-only Fmax
after A14 or after A19 falls below **60 MHz**, stop and read the timing report
before continuing, because §9 still needs headroom for the coprocessor on top.

Note that DMIPS/MHz, CoreMark/MHz and IPC are all per-MHz and are **immune** to
whatever Fmax does. Only the absolute Dhrystones/second and CoreMark score move.
Do not let an Fmax regression be reported as a performance regression, or the
reverse.

### 3.5 Everything this touches

Checked by search, not by memory. Anything not on this list should be treated as
a surprise worth stopping for.

**RTL** — `rv32i_pkg.sv` (opcode/ALU-op enums), `rvntt_decode.sv`,
`rvntt_hazard.sv` (multi-cycle stall), `rvntt_core.sv` (EX wiring, redirect
path), `rvntt_csr.sv` (`misa` bit 12), `rvntt_forward.sv` (no partial results),
plus new `rvntt_muldiv.sv`, a dedicated address adder for A17, and `rvntt_bpred.sv`
for A19.

**Model** — `model/rv32i_ref.py` extended to decode `M` (permitted addition; see
§2). The four frozen files are untouched.

**Verification** — `tb/cosim/gen_random_prog.py` (emit `M` with controlled
density), `tb/cosim/cycle_model.py` (§3.3), `tb/cosim/test_cosim_a5.py`,
`tb/cosim/test_riscv_tests.py` (add the `rv32um` suite; its `ISA` constant),
`tb/cosim/test_kyber_kat_spike.py` and `test_spike_xkntt.py` (`--isa` strings),
`tb/cocotb/test_decode_cocotb.py` (the A3 10⁶-word decoder equivalence sweep now
covers `M` encodings), `tb/riscof/rvntt/rvntt_isa.yaml` (`misa` bitmask and ISA
string), `tb/formal/run_riscv_formal.py` (§3.2), `tb/mutate/run_mutation.py`,
`tb/run_regress.py`.

**Software and build** — `sw/kyber/Makefile` (`-march`, and the dual baseline of

`docs/kyber-backends.md`.

**Unchanged, deliberately** — `docs/isa-spec.md` (disjoint opcode space; the only
`Xkntt`-adjacent fact that moves is `misa`, which the spec mentions only in its
exception table), `toolchain/` (no fork touched, no new pin), and every `Xkntt`
encoding.

## 4. Track A additions

### A14 — Multi-cycle EX, and the M datapath

**Build the mechanism before the instructions.** §8 I1 needs a multi-cycle EX
functional unit for `Xkntt` Tier 1 and offers two designs: **(a)** stall IF/ID/EX
for `L−1` cycles, simple and obviously correct; **(b)** fully pipeline it with
forwarding from each sub-stage, much better throughput and much more hazard
logic. Its recommendation is to build (a) first. **Build (a) here, generically —
a `busy`/`done` handshake from an arbitrary EX-resident unit, with the latency a
property of the unit and not of `M`.** I1 then inherits it instead of building a
second mechanism, and the first thing to exercise it is a standard, independently
verifiable extension rather than a custom one nobody else can check.

**Build:**

- `MUL`, `MULH`, `MULHSU`, `MULHU`. All four need the full 64-bit product, so
  build one 32×32→64 unit and select halves. On DSP48E1 (25×18) that is at most
  four slices. Pipeline it 3–4 stages and let Vivado pack `AREG`/`BREG`/`MREG`/
  `PREG` — the same advice §B1 gives for the Montgomery multiplier, and for the
  same reason. **Verify DSP inference by reading the synthesis report, not by
  assuming it**, exactly as §B1 insists.
- `DIV`, `DIVU`, `REM`, `REMU`. Iterative, fixed latency. Radix-2 restoring is 32
  cycles plus fixup and is obviously correct; build that first and measure before
  considering radix-4. **The latency must be data-independent** — not for the
  side-channel reason §B4 gives, but because a data-dependent latency makes the
  cycle model of §3.3 unbuildable.
- The three edge cases the specification mandates, which are where every
  implementation gets it wrong:
  - **Divide by zero**: quotient = all ones (−1 signed), remainder = dividend.
  - **Signed overflow**, `−2³¹ ÷ −1`: quotient = `−2³¹`, remainder = 0.
  - **No traps for either.** RISC-V division raises no exceptions. A core that
    traps here passes casual testing and fails compliance.
- `misa` gains bit 12. This is not cosmetic — `tb/riscof/rvntt/rvntt_isa.yaml`
  reads it to decide which tests to select.
- Decoder: `OP` with `funct7 = 0000001`. `dbg_unsupported` (LD0 red on the board)
  must not fire on any of the eight.
- Forwarding: the result is valid only in the final EX cycle. **Confirm no
  forwarding path can observe a partial product**, which is the characteristic
  failure of bolting a multi-cycle unit onto a forwarding network built for
  single-cycle results.

**Verify:** directed tests for every edge case above; `rv32um` from riscv-tests
(present in the pinned checkout, 8 tests, one per instruction); cosim against
Spike with `M` in the random program generator at high hazard density.

**Fault-inject:** break the divide-by-zero result, invert one `MULHSU` sign,
remove the signed-overflow special case, and let a consumer forward a partial
product. Each must be caught by something named in advance, per the existing
mutation manifest discipline. If any escapes, the stimulus is the gap, not the
checker — that has been true of every escape in this project so far.

**Realistic expectation:** Dhrystone and CoreMark both improve, because both call
`__divsi3`/`__mulsi3` today. The NTT instruction count should fall by ~6.25×, to
the 23,795 measured on Spike. If it does not, the multiplier is not being
selected and something is still calling libgcc.

**Done when:** all 8 `rv32um` tests pass on the RTL, 1000 random programs containing `M` at
high hazard density are byte-identical against Spike, and every mandated edge
case has a directed test that fails when that case is broken.

### A15 — M compliance and formal

**Build/run:**

- **RISCOF.** Change `rvntt_isa.yaml`'s ISA string to `RV32IMZicsr` and its
  `misa` reset value and bitmask to include bit 12 (`0x40000100` becomes
  `0x40001100`). That file's own header says what happens next: *"claim M and
  the multiply tests are selected"* — which is now the intent rather than the
  warning. The suite is present in the pinned checkout at
  `riscv-test-suite/rv32i_m/M/` and is **8 tests** (`mul`, `mulh`, `mulhsu`,
  `mulhu`, `div`, `divu`, `rem`, `remu`). Save the HTML report; keep the
  existing exclusions (`pmp` by name; the branch pin on `old-framework-3.x`)
  exactly as they are.
- **riscv-formal.** Add the `M` instruction models and resolve the depth problem
  by §3.2, in that order of preference. Whatever the outcome, **write down what
  is and is not proved**, in the same form `rtl/core/CLAUDE.md` already uses for
  `dmem`, CSRs and the `Xkntt` encodings.
- **The random program generator.** Add `M` opcodes with a `--mul-density` knob,
  mirroring the existing `--raw-density` / `--load-use-density` /
  `--branch-density`. Include operands that hit the edge cases: zero divisors,
  `−2³¹`, and `−1`. A generator that emits only benign operands will never
  produce the two special cases, and they are the ones that are wrong.
- **`model/rv32i_ref.py`.** Extend to decode `M`. Then re-run A3's 10⁶-word
  decoder equivalence sweep: it now covers `M` encodings, and it must still show
  the RTL and Python decoders agreeing on strict reserved-field behaviour for
  every `funct7` outside the three legal values.
- **`tb/cosim/cycle_model.py`.** Add the `Σ(latency − 1)` term. This is the easy
  half of §3.3; the hard half is A19's.

**Done when:** RISCOF passes the RV32IM suite with the report committed; the
formal result is either proved or recorded with its boundary stated; the A3
decoder sweep passes over the extended instruction set; and the A5 cycle-span
check passes with `M` in the stream.

### A16 — RV32IM on hardware: re-measurement and the dual baseline

**Build/run:**

- **Re-run A12's Fmax binary search.** *The A12 number does not carry.* A12
  established that ±0.4 ns of spread comes from placement alone — correcting two
  output pins, with no logical content, cost 3 MHz — and A14 is a real
  logic change. Constrain `T` in the XDC, implement to routed, read WNS
  **post-route**, bisect. Do not report `1/(T − WNS)` from a passing run.
- **Re-run A13 on hardware.** Dhrystone, CoreMark, IPC, three separate
  programming passes, exact-equality reproducibility. The existing harness does
  this unattended.
- **The dual baseline — the point of the whole exercise.** Build the ML-KEM
  software NTT backend twice, `-march=rv32i` and `-march=rv32im`, and run
  **both on the RV32IM hardware**. With `M` in the core the old baseline is still
  measurable, on real silicon, so §10 M2 and M3 can quote the honest number *and*
  show the other one beside it rather than choosing between them. Record the
  ratio measured here — 148,645 versus 23,795 instructions per `ntt()` — as the
  cross-check.
- Update the milestone table, `rtl/soc/CLAUDE.md`, `rtl/core/CLAUDE.md` and
  `docs/a13-benchmarks.md`, and tag.

**Done when:** **M7.1** is recorded, with the RV32I figures preserved rather than
overwritten. This step is deliberately placed before any performance work: it
fixes the number that §10 M2 and M3 depend on, and it is claimable even if
everything after it is deferred.

### A17 — Fmax recovery: address generation off the store path

**Not in the original plan, and it is here because A12's explanation of its own
Fmax number was wrong.** A12 concluded the design is slow because it is spread
across 32 BRAM tiles. Reading the post-route path hop by hop says otherwise:

| segment | delay | share |
|---|---|---|
| EX/MEM `rd_addr` → forwarding mux → `ex_alu_b` | 4.56 ns | 34% |
| `ex_alu_b` → CARRY4 → **ALU result mux, 4× LUT6** → `ex_addr_misaligned` | 4.44 ns | 33% |
| misalign → `ex_trap7_out` (**fanout 245**) → `dmem_be` | 2.14 ns | 16% |
| `dmem_be` → `ram_be` → BRAM `WEA` | 1.32 ns | 10% |

The core-to-BRAM crossing is under a fifth. About half is a logical dependency
chain, and the route dominance is largely a *consequence* of that chain being
long enough that the placer cannot keep it local.

**Build, in this order, and measure each one on its own.** Bundling them makes
the result unattributable, which is the same mistake as reporting a predictor's
payoff without a baseline.

1. **A dedicated address adder.** `ex_addr_misaligned` is `|ex_alu_y[1:0]` —
   bits read off the **muxed** ALU output, so the whole four-level
   operation-select mux sits in front of the alignment check, which gates the
   trap, which gates the byte enables. A memory address is always `rs1 + imm`;
   it is never a shift, an AND or a SUB. A separate adder feeding `dmem_addr`
   and the misalign check bypasses that mux entirely. Expect **2–3 ns**. This is
   the whole reason the step exists.
2. **`MAX_FANOUT` on the trap net.** `ex_trap7_out` drives 245 loads. §9 already
   lists the technique. Expect 0.3–0.5 ns.
3. **An implementation strategy sweep.** A12 used *default*. §9 suggests
   `Performance_ExplorePostRoutePhysOpt`. Free, typically 5–8%, and it costs
   only Vivado time.
4. **A memory-size experiment.** 128 KB is 32 BRAM tiles; the benchmark image is
   21.8 KB and ML-KEM-768's keys and ciphertext are a few KB more. Try 64 KB
   (16 tiles) and measure. **Adopt it only if the payoff is large**, because
   §1.4 specifies 128 KB — if adopted, this becomes a third OVERRIDES entry in
   §2 and the reason must be recorded there, not left in a commit message.

**The done-when that makes this honest: not one cycle count may change.**
Every item above is a purely combinational or physical restructuring, and
Dhrystone and CoreMark on this SoC are deterministic to the cycle. So the
benchmark output before and after must be **identical except for the clock
frequency** — same `dhry_cycles`, same `cm_cycles`, same `minstret`, same CRCs.
If a cycle count moves, the change was not a timing change and it does not
belong in this step. A17 has no IPC cost by construction, and this is how that
claim is checked rather than asserted.

**Verify:** item 1 touches the A9 trap invariant — that every trap resolves in
EX and a faulting store is suppressed before its byte enables leave EX — which
already has an unusually strong net: riscv-formal, RISCOF's `privilege` suite,
the `rv32mi` misaligned load/store tests, and A9's directed tests. Run all of
them, and fault-inject the new adder: make it drop the carry into bit 2, and
make the misalign check read the ALU output again instead of the new one. The
second must be caught by timing, not function, which is worth knowing.

**Realistic expectation:** 85–90 MHz likely, ~100 MHz plausible but not assured.
The original A12 range was 70–110 MHz and we sit at the bottom of it; the point
of this step is to stop being there before adding a predictor, not to chase a
round number.

**Stop rule:** if a lever costs cycles, reject it — it is a microarchitecture
change wearing a timing change's clothes, and it belongs in its own step with
its own before/after. If after all four the core is still under 75 MHz, stop and
report rather than reaching for pipeline surgery; §9 needs headroom, not a
rebuild.

**Done when:** Fmax is re-measured by A12's binary search on post-route WNS, each
lever's contribution is reported separately, and the benchmark cycle counts are
bit-identical to A16's.

---

### A18 — The stall attribution instrument (do this before the predictor)

Deliberately structured like A5, and for the same reason: A5 built the commit-log
tracer *before* the hazard logic because you cannot debug what you cannot see.
**You cannot honestly report a predictor's payoff without an instrument that
measured the baseline first**, and "we added a predictor and the benchmark got
faster" is not an attribution.

**Build:** a profiling harness over the RTL that counts, across a delimited
region: retired instructions, load-use stall cycles, multi-cycle EX stall cycles,
`ex_redirect` pulses, and a classification of retired control transfers into
taken branch / not-taken branch / `JAL` / `JALR`.

**The check that makes it trustworthy** is that the accounting closes:

    cycles = retired + load_use_stalls + muldiv_stalls + 2 × redirects

**The residual must be exactly zero.** If it is not, either the instrument or the
core is wrong, and you must find out which before believing any number that comes
out of it. On the RV32I core this identity already closes with residual 0 over
both benchmarks, which is what makes the table in §1 usable.

**Baseline already measured** (RV32I, no `M`, no predictor) — the §1 table. Note
it will shift after A14 (fewer instructions, a different mix, a new stall
category) and must be UNCHANGED by A17, which is that step's own done-when. **Retake it after A14 and before A19**, or the predictor's delta will
be contaminated by the multiplier's.

**Done when:** the residual is zero on both benchmarks with `M` present, and the
instrument runs in the regression rather than living in a scratch directory —
the same standard `tb/mutate/run_mutation.py` was held to.

---

### A19 — Branch prediction: 2-bit bimodal, 64-entry BTB, 8-entry RAS

This is §A13's "optional improvement loop", promoted to a specified step because
A18's measurement says control flow is ~90% of the lost cycles. Three deviations
from the original one-line description, each earned by that measurement.

**Build:**

- **A 2-bit bimodal predictor.** The pattern-history table is 2 bits per entry
  and is therefore cheap; size it independently of the BTB rather than assuming
  they must match.
- **A 64-entry BTB, direct-mapped and tagged, looked up in IF with the fetch
  address.** *This is the one design decision that determines whether the
  projected numbers are real.* A hit must redirect the fetch in the same cycle so
  a correctly-predicted taken branch costs **zero** bubbles. If the lookup lands
  in ID instead, every predicted-taken branch costs one bubble and roughly half
  the payoff disappears — and the design will still look correct, because it is.
- **An 8-entry return-address stack.** Not in the original description, and the
  measurement is why: `JALR` is 32,004 of Dhrystone's 190,008 redirects — 16
  returns per iteration, because upstream riscv-tests builds Dhrystone with the
  no-inline pragma. A BTB stores one target per PC and predicts returns badly,
  since the same `ret` goes to different callers. Push/pop on the architectural
  return hints (`rd`/`rs1` being `x1` or `x5`), not on a guess.
- **Register the BTB's output, not just its input.** The bullet above says
  *where* the lookup happens; this says how to stop it costing Fmax, which is the
  one real timing risk in this document. Done naively, `PC → BTB index → RAM read
  → tag compare → next-PC mux → PC register` is a new critical path in IF, on a
  design A17 has just spent effort giving margin to. Read the BTB one cycle
  ahead and register the hit, target and prediction, so that only the final mux
  is left in the PC path. This does **not** cost the zero-bubble property — the
  branch instruction is fetched normally either way and it is its *successor*
  that comes from the predicted target — it only moves the read off the critical
  path. If the naive version is built and Fmax drops, this is the first thing to
  change.
- **Recovery reuses the existing EX redirect path**, which already flushes both
  younger slots. The predictor must be architecturally invisible: it changes when
  instructions are fetched, never which ones retire.
- **Initialise the counters weakly-not-taken and require a BTB tag hit before
  predicting taken.** See the downside term below.

**The downside term, stated so that it is measured rather than discovered.**
74,001 not-taken branches per 2,000 Dhrystone runs are **free today** under
static not-taken. Every one the predictor gets wrong costs two cycles it does not
currently cost. The net is strongly positive, but a predictor with a careless
reset policy could plausibly make CoreMark *slower*, and the report must show the
regression term alongside the improvement, not just the net.

**Write the specification before the RTL.** §3.3 requires the cycle model to
simulate the predictor from its specification rather than from its
implementation. That is only possible if the index function, the tag comparison,
the replacement policy, the counter update rule and the RAS push/pop conditions
are all written down precisely enough to be implemented twice, independently.
Treat that document as a deliverable of this step.

**Verify:**

- **riscv-formal is the right net here and it already exists.** `pc_fwd`,
  `pc_bwd`, `causal` and `unique` are precisely the checks that see a
  speculation bug, and they must still pass at the same depth. A predictor that
  changes architectural PC behaviour cannot survive them.
- Cosim: 1000 random programs at high branch density, byte-identical against
  Spike.
- The cycle model of §3.3, predicting the span with mispredicts.
- **Fault-inject:** break the BTB tag comparison so it predicts on any index
  match; invert the 2-bit counter saturation; break the RAS push condition;
  predict taken from a cold entry. Each must be caught, and at least one must be
  caught by the *cycle model* rather than by a correctness check — a predictor
  bug that is architecturally invisible is exactly the class the commit-log diff
  cannot see, which is the reason the cycle model exists at all.

**Realistic expectation**, projected from A18's measured counts:

| | measured today | conservative | central | with RAS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DMIPS/MHz | 0.7306 | 0.892 | 0.913 | 0.939 |
| CoreMark/MHz | 0.9607 | ~1.19 | ~1.26 | ~1.31 |
| IPC (Dhrystone) | 0.7227 | 0.883 | 0.904 | 0.929 |

**+22% to +31%**, and the first time both figures land inside §A13's stated
bands. These are projections from measured counts, not guesses — but they assume
`M` is *not* present. After A14 the instruction mix changes and the branch
fraction with it, so **retake the baseline and recompute the projection before
believing the delta**.

**If you measure less than +15%, the BTB is almost certainly being read in ID
rather than IF.** Check that before looking anywhere else.

**Done when:** measured before and after with A18's instrument, on hardware;
riscv-formal still passes at the same depth; cosim still passes; the reported
delta includes the not-taken regression term; and Fmax, DMIPS/MHz, CoreMark/MHz
and IPC are re-measured by A16's procedure across three programming passes.
**M7.2 is claimed here.**

## 5. Risk register additions

In the format of §11, and additive to it.

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| `DIV` latency puts riscv-formal past a depth that converges | Medium — a verification gap in a milestone that already passed | §3.2's three routes, in order. Never lower the global depth to accommodate it. |
| The cycle model has to be taught the predictor and stops being independent | Medium — the check quietly becomes self-fulfilling | Model the predictor from its written specification, never from RTL state. Make the specification a deliverable of A19. |
| A14 + A19 together push Fmax below what §9 needs for the coprocessor | Medium — discovered at M15, far from the cause | Re-measure after **each** step, not once at the end. Stop at 60 MHz per §A12 and read the report. |
| A predictor makes CoreMark slower via the 74k free not-taken branches | Low, but embarrassing if unreported | Weakly-not-taken initialisation, tag hit required before predicting taken, and report the regression term separately. |
| The RV32IM numbers silently replace the RV32I ones in the write-up | Medium — the `m7` tag stops matching the prose | M7 and its tag are explicitly not reopened. §10 M1 reports both. |
| A multi-cycle EX unit is built twice, once for `M` and once for `Xkntt` | Medium — two hazard mechanisms is how you get a hazard bug | A14 builds it generically. §8 I1 inherits it; this is written into §2. |
| A17's address adder breaks the A9 trap invariant | High — a store that should have faulted reaches the RAM | It is the best-covered invariant in the project: riscv-formal, RISCOF `privilege`, the `rv32mi` misaligned tests and A9's directed tests all watch it. Run all four, and fault-inject the adder. |
| A17's levers get bundled and the result is unattributable | Low, but it wastes the step | Measure each of the four separately. The cycle counts must be bit-identical throughout; that is the step's done-when. |
| The memory-size experiment is adopted quietly and §1.4 silently stops being true | Medium — the plan and the design diverge with nothing saying so | Adopt only on a large measured payoff, and record it as a third OVERRIDES row in §2. |

## 6. Milestone additions

Added to §12's sequence. `RISC-V_NTT.txt` is not edited; these live here.

- [ ] **M7.1** — **RV32IM core: `M` verified and the benchmark re-measured.**
  *Satisfied by A14, A15 and A16.* RISCOF passes the RV32IM suite with the report
  committed; riscv-formal's `M` models either pass or their boundary is recorded;
  1000 random programs containing `M` at high hazard density are byte-identical
  against Spike; and Fmax, DMIPS/MHz, CoreMark/MHz and IPC are re-measured on
  hardware across three passes. **The dual software NTT baseline —
  `-march=rv32i` and `-march=rv32im`, both run on the RV32IM board — is
  recorded, because that is the number §10 M2 and M3 depend on.** M7's RV32I
  figures are preserved, not replaced.

- [ ] **M7.2** — **Core performance: Fmax recovered and branch prediction
  measured.** *Satisfied by A17, A18 and A19.* A17's four levers are each
  measured separately, with the benchmark cycle counts bit-identical before and
  after — the proof that it was a timing change and nothing else. The predictor,
  BTB and RAS are specified precisely enough for the cycle model to simulate
  them independently; riscv-formal still passes at the same depth; cosim still
  passes; and the before/after is measured on hardware with A18's instrument,
  reported with the not-taken regression term shown separately and with the
  residual stall attribution for what remains. The core-only Fmax baseline for
  §9 is updated to this number.

**A17 has no milestone of its own**, deliberately. The original plan gives
fourteen Track A steps eight milestones; not every step earns one. A17 is an
enabler measured inside M7.2, and giving it its own milestone would invite
claiming it before the predictor it exists to make room for.

**Why two milestones and not one.** They were asked for as one. They are split
because M7.1 must remain claimable if everything after it is deferred or
abandoned: the `M` extension fixes a correctness-of-measurement problem that §10
depends on, while Fmax recovery and the predictor are performance improvements
that §10 does not depend on. A single milestone would make the necessary work
hostage to the optional work. Collapse them if you disagree — nothing else in
this document depends on the split.

**Sequencing, and why it is this way.** A14→A16 first because §8 I1 needs the
multi-cycle EX mechanism regardless, and building it under a standard extension
means it arrives verified by RISCOF, riscv-formal and `rv32um` before `Xkntt` —
which has no external reference — depends on it. A17 before A19 because the
predictor is the one change here with real Fmax risk and it should land on a
design that has margin. A18 before A19 for the same reason A5 came before the
hazard logic: you cannot report a payoff you did not measure first.
