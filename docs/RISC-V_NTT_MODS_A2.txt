# B, Zicond, Zkt, Zkr, Counters and 110 MHz — Track A Modifications, Round 2

Companion to `RISC-V_NTT.txt`, which it never edits, and to
`RISC-V_NTT_MODS_A.txt`, which it also never edits. Same target platform, same
budget: 63,400 LUTs, 126,800 FFs, 240 DSP48E1, 135 × 36 Kb BRAM, 100 MHz
oscillator, `xc7a100tcsg324-1` at **-1** speed grade. Track A as extended by
`MODS_A` is finished — A14 through A19, milestones M7.1 and M7.2, tagged
`m7.1-rv32im-hw` and `m7.2-bpred-77mhz`. This document adds **A20 through A30**
and milestones **M7.3, M7.4 and M7.5**.

It exists for a different reason than `MODS_A` did. `MODS_A` existed because a
measurement said the original scope was one extension short of the baseline the
original document itself assumed — it was a correction. This one is not a
correction. It is a deliberate widening of the core, taken while the coprocessor
does not yet exist, because four of the five things in it get harder or become
impossible once it does.

---

## 0. How to read this against RISC-V_NTT.txt and MODS_A

**`RISC-V_NTT.txt` is never modified.** **`RISC-V_NTT_MODS_A.txt` is never
modified either** — it is the record of what A14–A19 were planned to be, and the
two tags above are the record of what that produced. Everything here is
additive.

The same three markers are used, and they mean the same things:

- **OVERRIDES §x** — the original text is now wrong and this replaces it.
- **EXTENDS §x** — the original stands and something is added on top. The
  original's "done when" remains satisfied by what was already built.
- **CLARIFIES §x** — the original was ambiguous or rested on an unstated
  assumption. Nothing changes; the assumption is written down.

A marker may now name **either** source document, so every row in §2 says which
one it is amending. Rows that amend `MODS_A` are marked `MODS_A §x`; rows with a
bare `§x` amend `RISC-V_NTT.txt`.

**The numbering hazard restated, because this round makes it worse.**
`RISC-V_NTT.txt` uses `M1`–`M5` in §10 as *measurement* headings and `M0`–`M16`
in §12 as *milestones*. `MODS_A` added milestones `M7.1` and `M7.2`. This
document adds milestones `M7.3`, `M7.4` and `M7.5` — all in **§12's** sequence.
Where §10's measurements are amended they are named "§10 M2", never bare.

**A second numbering hazard, new to this round and easy to trip on.** This
document adds ISA extensions whose names collide with the plan's own section
letters. `§B6` is a *plan section about the coprocessor*; `Zbb` is an
*extension*. The bare letter `B` in this document always means the **ratified B
extension** (Zba + Zbb + Zbs); the plan's coprocessor track is always written
`§B<n>` with a section number.

## 1. Why this exists

Five reasons, in the order they were arrived at rather than the order they are
built.

**The first: Keccak is the half of ML-KEM that nothing in this project touches,
and §10 M3 asks for it by name.** §10 M3 says, in the plan's own words, that on
a small core with no Keccak acceleration *"the SHAKE128/SHAKE256/SHA3 work
dominates ML-KEM"*, and asks for the breakdown before and after. There is no
ratified RISC-V Keccak extension. There is, however, `rori` and `andn` — the
two operations a Keccak round is built out of — and they are in Zbb, which is
ratified, which Spike executes natively, for which riscv-formal ships instruction
models, and for which the pinned arch-test branch ships a compliance suite. **The
acceleration path for the half of ML-KEM the NTT does not touch is a standard
extension, already supported by every reference this project checks itself
against.** That is an unusually cheap thing to leave on the table.

**The second: every attribution number in this project is inferred from
simulation.** A18 built a Verilator observer rather than hardware counters, on
purpose, so that the design A17 was tuning was not perturbed by the instrument
measuring it. That was right then, and it closed to residual exactly zero. But it
means the identity `cycles = retired + load-use + multi-cycle EX + 2 × redirects`
is validated on hardware against **two** counters — `mcycle` and `minstret` — and
every one of its four terms is a simulation result. `Zihpm` makes the terms
first-hand. It also makes A19's payoff measurable on the board rather than
projected onto it, and the same counters are what §10 M2's data-movement cost
will need when the coprocessor exists.

**The third: the core is slow, and it is slow for a reason that is now
measured rather than guessed.** 77.501 MHz on a part whose speed grade allows a
great deal more, at 3.5% utilisation, with **67.6% of the critical path in route
delay**. That is not congestion. §3.4 reads the post-route path hop by hop and
finds two structural causes and one physical one, none of which has been
attacked. A17 raised Fmax 17.3% by taking a single adder off a mux; there is more
of that available.

**The fourth: a data-independence guarantee is worth more before the
coprocessor than after.** §B8 already requires the coprocessor to be
constant-time and says so in security terms. `Zkt` is the same claim for the
*core*, it is ratified, and this core is unusually well placed to make it: one
fixed-latency multiplier, one divider that is data-independent by construction,
and everything else combinational. Making the claim now means the coprocessor
arrives into a machine that already has it, rather than being the first thing
asked to justify it.

**The fifth: `Zkr` is the one item here that is easy to over-claim, which is
why it is specified rather than left to a later "add an RNG" step.** ML-KEM needs
randomness. The plan's KATs need determinism. Those are not in conflict but they
are easy to wire together wrongly, and the failure mode — a KAT that passes
because the entropy source was quietly bypassed, or a keygen that is
deterministic in the field — is silent in both directions.

**What is deliberately NOT here: the C extension.** §3.1 gives the reasoning at
length. It is fenced off, not deferred by omission.

**The `misa` note, REWRITTEN AFTER A21 MEASURED IT.** This paragraph originally
said that `misa` bit 1 must be set, and that not setting it *"silently drops the
entire B compliance suite"*. **Both halves turned out to be wrong**, and the
correction is worth more than the original claim:

- **arch-test does not select suites from `misa`.** It selects them by regex on
  the ISA string — `check ISA:=regex(.*I.*Zbb.*)` — so
  `RV32IMZicsr_Zba_Zbb_Zbkb_Zbs` selects all 34 B and Zbkb tests with bit 1
  clear. Nothing is dropped.
- **riscv-config 3.18.3 cannot express `B` at all.** Every spelling of the
  letter — `RV32IMB…`, `RV32IMB_Zicsr…` — is rejected as *"does not match
  accepted canonical ordering"*, and it derives the expected `misa` from the
  single-letter extensions only. Setting bit 1 makes the config invalid, and
  RISCOF then runs **nothing**.
- It also requires `Zbs` to come **after** `Zbkb` in the string, which is why
  the canonical form here is `Zba_Zbb_Zbkb_Zbs` and not alphabetical.

**So `misa.B` is deliberately not asserted, and that is a recorded boundary in
the sense M5 and M6 use the word.** The extension is implemented, selected,
compliance-tested (29/29 ratified-B, 5/5 Zbkb) and formally verified; only the
reporting bit is withheld, because asserting it would break the tooling that
proves the rest. `rvntt_csr.sv` carries the reasoning next to the constant.

The original paragraph's last sentence stands unchanged and is the reason this
was caught at all: the reset value, the YAML's `reset-val`, the YAML's
`bitmask`, and the `--isa` strings in `tb/cosim/` move in **one commit** or they
do not move.

## 3. Conflict analysis

`MODS_A` §3 looked for things that become intractable later and found four
non-problems, two genuinely hard parts and one budget. This round reaches into
Track B and beyond, so the same exercise is done wider: **six checked
non-problems, three genuinely hard parts, one cross-track obligation, one set of
moving baselines and two budgets.**

Everything below marked *"verified"* was checked by running something. The
commands are in the step that depends on the answer.

### 3.1 What is *not* a conflict, and why — each of these was checked

**Opcode space. Verified.** The catastrophe would be a new extension landing on
`Xkntt`. It does not, and this was checked rather than assumed:

| Space | Opcode | Owner | Touched this round? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `custom-0` | `0b0001011` (`0x0B`) | `Xkntt` Tier 1 | **No** |
| `custom-1` | `0b0101011` (`0x2B`) | `Xkntt` Tier 2 | **No** |
| `custom-2` | `0b1011011` (`0x5B`) | free | No — see below |
| `custom-3` | `0b1111011` (`0x7B`) | free | No |

Everything added this round lands in `OP` (`0x33`), `OP-IMM` (`0x13`) or
`SYSTEM` (`0x73`), all of them standard. **`custom-2` is noted as the natural
home for a future `Xkeccak`** — mentioned so the space is not spent by accident,
and **not reserved, not encoded, and not implemented**. Leaving the door open
costs a sentence; walking through it is a different project.

**The strict reserved-field rule survives, and this is where it would erode if
it were going to.** `MODS_A` added exactly one legal `funct7` to `OP` — `0000001`
for `M`. This round adds nine, and **the list below is
read off Appendix A's mechanically-generated table, not written from memory** —
the distinction between `OP` and `OP-IMM` is exactly where a hand-written version
goes wrong.

In **`OP` (`0x33`)**, nine `funct7` values become legal: `0010000` (Zba
`sh1add`/`sh2add`/`sh3add`), `0100000` (**already legal** for `SUB`/`SRA`, now
also `andn`/`orn`/`xnor`), `0000101` (`min`/`minu`/`max`/`maxu` — this is also
`clmul`'s group, and **`clmul` itself is not implemented**, see below),
`0110000` (`rol`/`ror`), `0000100` (`zext.h`/`pack`/`packh`), `0010100`
(`bset`), `0100100` (`bclr`/`bext`), `0110100` (`binv`), and `0000111` (Zicond
`czero.eqz`/`czero.nez`).

In **`OP-IMM` (`0x13`)** the single-operand and shift-immediate forms carry their
own strict `imm[11:5]` decode: `0110000` (`rori`, and the `clz`/`ctz`/`cpop`/
`sext.b`/`sext.h` group), `0010100` (`orc.b`/`bseti`), `0100100`
(`bclri`/`bexti`), `0110100` (`rev8`/`binvi`/`brev8`), and `0000100`
(`zip`/`unzip`).

**Every other `funct7` in `OP` remains illegal**, and every other `imm[11:5]` in
the `OP-IMM` shift forms remains illegal.

**One decode trap, visible in Appendix A and easy to miss.** `clz`, `ctz`,
`cpop`, `sext.b` and `sext.h` **share `opcode`, `funct3` and `imm[11:5]`
entirely** — they are `0x60059513`, `0x60159513`, `0x60259513`, `0x60459513` and
`0x60559513`, distinguished **only by the `rs2` field** (`imm[4:0]` = 0, 1, 2, 4,
5). Values 3, 6 and 7 in that field are **reserved and must trap**, and a decoder
that treats the field as a don't-care will accept three illegal encodings while
passing every functional test, because no functional test ever emits them. This
is precisely the class A3's sweep exists to catch. The point of A3's 10⁶-word RTL-vs-Python decoder equivalence sweep is
that a lax and a strict decoder disagree on exactly these words; **A21 extends
that sweep to the new encodings rather than trusting the paragraph above.**

**Toolchain. Verified, not assumed** — the same check `MODS_A` §3.1 ran, re-run
for the new strings. `riscv-none-elf-gcc 15.2.0` compiles
`-march=rv32im_zba_zbb_zbs_zicsr`, `…_zbkb_zicsr` and `…_zbkb_zicond_zicsr`, all
with `-mabi=ilp32`, without error. **`-print-libgcc-file-name` for the B build
resolves to the existing `rv32im/ilp32/libgcc.a`** — the same multilib the
project already uses. So there is **no new multilib, no toolchain rebuild, and no
new pin**. libgcc itself stays a plain RV32IM build and simply does not use B
instructions internally, which costs nothing that matters: after `M`, libgcc is
no longer on the hot path of either benchmark.

**Spike. Verified, and the check was fault-injected.** `spike --isa=` accepts
`rv32im_zba_zbb_zbs_zbkb_zicond_zicsr_zicntr_xkntt0p1`, and accepts `zkt` and
`zkr`. The fork already contains `czero_eqz.h`, `czero_nez.h`, the full Zb
instruction set, and `seed_csr_t` with `CSR_SEED = 0x015` behind `EXT_ZKR`.
**The acceptance test was fault-injected**: `rv32im_znonsense_zicsr` and
`rv32im_zbq_zicsr` are both rejected with *"unsupported extension"*, so the
ACCEPT results are real and not a test that passes on everything. **No fork
change is needed**, so `toolchain/patches.sh export spike` is unaffected and
`patches_in_sync` stays green. Only the `--isa` strings in `tb/cosim/*` change.

**riscv-formal ships usable Zb models — this was the item most expected to be
absent, and it is present in full.** The instruction models exist for every one
of the 34 instructions this round adds, **and so do pre-built ISA bundles**:
`isa_rv32ib.txt` (66 instructions = RV32I + Zba + Zbb + Zbs, confirming ratified
B's composition from a local source rather than from memory) and
`isa_rv32iZbkb.txt` (49 = RV32I + Zbkb). `generate.py` defines `Zba`, `Zbb`,
`Zbs`, `Zbkb` as first-class groups and composes `rv32ib` from the first three.

**And they add no BMC depth pressure, which is the thing that made `M` hard.**
`MODS_A` §3.2's whole problem was that a 34-cycle iterative divider pushes the
required depth to ~46. **Every instruction added this round is single-cycle and
combinational** — `clz`, `ctz` and `cpop` included. The existing depth of 14
stands unchanged, and the only real work is that **no `isa_rv32imb` bundle
exists**, so the check list must be composed as the union of `rv32im`, `rv32ib`
and `rv32iZbkb` — mechanical, and A21's job.

**The arch-test branch has B coverage — also present, also expected to be
absent.** `toolchain/riscv-arch-test`, pinned to `old-framework-3.x`, ships
`riscv-test-suite/rv32i_m/B/` with **32 tests**, `Zicond/` with **2**
(`czero.eqz`, `czero.nez` — exactly the extension), and `K/` with **55**, of
which `pack`, `packh`, `brev8_32`, `zip` and `unzip` are the Zbkb instructions
that Zbb does not already cover.

**But the B suite is the OLD grouping, and this is a real trap rather than a
tidy result.** Its 32 tests include `clmul-01`, `clmulh-01` and `clmulr-01`,
which are **`Zbc`, not `B`** — ratified B is Zba + Zbb + Zbs and contains no
carry-less multiply. The suite predates that split. So selecting the B suite
selects three tests for an extension this round does not implement. There are two
honest responses and one dishonest one:

1. **Deselect `clmul*` by name and record the boundary**, exactly as M5 records
   the `pmp` exclusion and for exactly the same reason — riscf 1.25.3 ignores
   the `verify` clause these tests would otherwise use to deselect themselves.
   **This is the choice.** The result is reported as *29/29 of the ratified-B
   tests, with 3 `Zbc` tests excluded by name*, never as "32/32" and never as
   "B passes".
2. Implement `Zbc` as well. Rejected: a 32×32 carry-less multiplier is real area
   on a design whose Fmax is the point of the round, Keccak does not use it,
   ML-KEM does not use it, and `Zbc` is not in B.
3. Claim the B suite passes without saying which tests ran. **This is the
   failure this project has now seen four times** — A10's RISCOF exit code,
   A11's sby exit code, A14's `synth_ooc.sh` `DSP=0`, and A19's stale
   `bench_hardware` fixture. The shape is always the same: a report whose green
   is not about the thing it names.

**DSP budget.** Unchanged and still not close. `M` takes 4 DSP48E1; A25's 2-cycle
multiply changes *where the pipeline registers sit inside those DSPs*, not how
many there are. §B1's 3-per-Montgomery-multiplier plus 2 for Barrett still
projects ~65 of 240 at P=16. B adds none — no B instruction is a multiply.

**The C extension is fenced off, and here is the reasoning rather than an
omission.** It is genuinely tempting: code density feeds the memory-size Fmax
lever, and it is the one remaining standard extension with a large effect on this
workload. It is rejected for this round, on four grounds, three of which are
specific to what has already been built:

- **It deletes a trap that two independent checkers currently watch.** With `C`,
  `IALIGN` becomes 16, and `ex_target_misaligned = ex_ctrl_xfer &&
  ex_jump_target[1]` — A9's instruction-address-misaligned trap — **stops
  existing**. That is not a modification to the trap logic; it is the removal of
  a behaviour that riscv-formal's `pc_fwd`/`pc_bwd`, RISCOF's `privilege` suite
  and A9's directed tests all assert today. Every one of those has to be
  re-taught simultaneously, and until they are, the safest-looking outcome —
  they all still pass — would mean they are testing a machine that no longer
  exists.
- **It breaks A19's BTB indexing by construction.** The BTB entry is `{valid,
  tag[21:0], kind[1:0], cnt[1:0], target[31:2]}` and both the index and the tag
  are derived from a **word** address, which is what makes a tag hit provably the
  same instruction that allocated the entry — the property A19's specification
  leans on to argue that a taken prediction on a non-control-transfer cannot
  happen. Halfword PCs invalidate that argument, not just that arithmetic.
- **It puts a decompressor in IF, which is where the Fmax goal is already
  fighting.** §3.4's path ends at `id_ex_q[pc]` through the redirect. Adding a
  16-bit realignment buffer and an expander in front of decode is logic on the
  fetch path in the same round that is trying to shorten it.
- **The density argument does not actually bind.** The benchmark image is
  21.8 KB in 128 KB. Density would buy a smaller image; the reason 128 KB is not
  being cut is not that the image is too big (§2), it is that the Tier-2 buffers
  are unsized. `C` does not help with that.

**If `C` is ever adopted it is its own late milestone with a full
re-measurement — never folded into a batch**, because it moves cycle counts, code
size, the trap surface, the predictor's indexing and `misa` simultaneously, and a
batched result would be unattributable across all five.

### 3.2 The cross-track obligation: the four-way agreement, and what Track B and C inherit

`CLAUDE.md` requires that **the RTL decoder, `model/isa/xkntt.py`, Spike and the
LLVM `SchedMachineModel` agree exactly, strict reserved fields included.** Three
of the four exist; the LLVM `SchedMachineModel` is §C4 and is not built.

**This round does not add a fifth party, and it does not touch `Xkntt`.** B,
Zicond, Zkr and Zihpm are standard: Spike knows them natively, LLVM knows them
natively, and none of them has an entry in `model/isa/xkntt.py` because none of
them is an `Xkntt` instruction. The agreement's *subject* is unchanged.

**What does change is the size of §C4's obligation, and Track B/C may be scoped
by an agent that does not know it.** So it is written here, in the document their
own scoping will be told to read:

> **To whoever scopes Track C's C4.** The `SchedMachineModel` you write must
> cover, in addition to `Xkntt`'s six instructions and `M`'s eight: the ~34 B and
> Zbkb instructions (all 1-cycle, all `IntALU`-class, and therefore cheap to
> model but **not** free to omit — an omitted instruction gets default
> scheduling, not zero scheduling), Zicond's two, and the `seed` CSR read, which
> is **not** a normal CSR read: it has a side effect, it may return
> `WAIT`/`BIST`, and software is expected to poll it. Modelling `csrrw seed` as a
> plain CSR access will produce a scheduler that hoists or CSEs a polling loop.
> §C3's warning about intrinsic attributes applies to it directly.
>
> **And the latency table in `CLAUDE.md` moves this round.** A25 changes `MUL`
> from 4 cycles to 2. `CLAUDE.md`'s rule is that RTL, Spike and the
> `SchedMachineModel` change **together, in one commit**. Spike is untimed and
> `M`'s RTL latency is not in `docs/isa-spec.md` — that table is `Xkntt`'s — so
> for `M` the rule reduces to: **the `SchedMachineModel`, when it is written,
> must be written against A25's 2-cycle multiply and not against A14's
> 4-cycle one.** There is no commit that can enforce this today because the file
> does not exist. This paragraph is the enforcement.

**`toolchain/llvm-project` is an empty, uninitialised checkout.** Nothing in this
round changes that, and nothing in this round depends on it.

### 3.3 Genuinely hard, first: the coprocessor's clock — decided, not deferred

This was named the most important cross-track question in the round. It has an
answer, and the answer is different for the two tiers.

**Tier 1 must share the core's clock. This is not a decision; it is a
consequence, and it follows from documents that are already frozen.**
`docs/isa-spec.md` is the frozen `Xkntt` contract, and it gives Tier-1
instructions latencies of **4 to 9 cycles** of EX occupancy — `kmm` 4, `kbfct` 5,
`kbfgs` 5, `kbmul0` 9, `kmac` 6, `kbmul1` 5. A Tier-1 instruction reads the
register file through the forwarding network, occupies EX, and writes back. **A
clock-domain crossing costs two to three flops of synchroniser in each
direction** — four to six cycles minimum, before the unit does any arithmetic,
and only if the request is a level rather than a pulse. `kmm`'s entire budget is
four. **A separate clock domain for Tier 1 would make the frozen latency table
unimplementable**, and that table is a contract the RTL decoder, Spike and the
LLVM scheduler are all required to agree on.

So: **Tier 1 is single-domain, and therefore the core's Fmax is a hard floor the
Tier-1 butterfly must meet.** §B9's criterion — *"the coprocessor's standalone
Fmax exceeds the core's Fmax"* — is the right shape for a bus slave and the wrong
shape for an EX-stage functional unit, because for Tier 1 it is not a comparison
between two independently-clocked things. It is a constraint on one thing.

**This is the direct conflict between goal 1 and the rest of the project, and it
is what the Fmax stop rule is anchored to.** Every megahertz added to the core is
a megahertz the Tier-1 Montgomery butterfly must also close at. Pushing the core
to 110 MHz and discovering at M15 that the butterfly closes at 95 would be
exactly the §9 failure mode — fired far from its cause, after both tracks are
built.

**So the evidence is brought forward.** A24 synthesises a representative Tier-1
butterfly out-of-context, at the target frequency, **before A26 and A27 commit
the core to a number**. That converts *"state precisely what evidence would
decide it and when that evidence arrives"* into evidence that arrives in this
round, for the cost of one Vivado OOC run. It is deliberately placed as the first
step of the performance phase rather than the last.

**Tier 2 may have its own domain, and that decision is deferred with a named
trigger.** Tier 2 is a block coprocessor behind `kntt.start`/`kntt.wait` with its
own RAM and, per §B7, a memory-mapped register interface. Its interface is a
register write and a poll against an operation of hundreds of cycles, so a
two-flop synchroniser is a rounding error rather than a latency contract. The
trigger is explicit: **if §B9's OOC sweep shows the P≥4 datapath closing below
the core's shipped Fmax, Tier 2 goes into its own domain and the register
interface gets a proper CDC** — not a re-timed core. Recorded here so that
decision is made against a measurement rather than under M15's schedule
pressure.

**One consequence worth stating now.** If Tier 2 does end up in its own domain,
§B7's `CYCLES` counter counts *coprocessor* cycles, and §10 M2's speedup must
then be computed in **time**, not cycles, or it will silently compare two
different clocks. §B7 does not say which, because when it was written there was
only one clock.

### 3.4 Genuinely hard, second: B and the Fmax goal want the same mux

**These two goals fight, and they fight in one specific place.** The fight is
avoidable, but only if the avoidance is designed in from the start — retrofitting
it after B is in the ALU means re-doing the step.

**The measured critical path today**, from
`fpga/build/fmax_a19/mhz_77.94/post_route_critical.rpt`, the closest failing
point to the shipped 77.501 MHz. Source `mem_wb_q_reg[rd_addr][1]`, destination
`id_ex_q_reg[pc][24]`, **12.954 ns, 19 logic levels, logic 4.202 ns (32.4%),
route 8.752 ns (67.6%)**:

| # | segment | signal reached | ~delay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MEM/WB `rd_addr` → forwarding compare → forwarding mux | `b[0]`, `uses_rs2` | 3.35 ns |
| 2 | operand select → **ALU adder**, 2 × CARRY4 | `data0[4]` | 2.67 ns |
| 3 | **ALU result mux**, LUT5 + 2 × LUT6 + LUT3 | `ex_alu_y[2]` | 1.55 ns |
| 4 | JALR target form → | `ex_jump_target[2]` | 1.13 ns |
| 5 | **mispredict compare**, LUT6 + 3 × CARRY4 | `ex_mispredict` | 2.03 ns |
| 6 | flush → IF/ID valid (fanout 131) → ID/EX `pc` | `id_ex_q[pc][24]` | 2.22 ns |

**Segment 3 is the ALU result mux, and the B extension's natural implementation
makes it four times wider.** `alu_op_e` is 4 bits with 11 members. B and Zbkb add
34 instructions; folding them into `rvntt_alu`'s `unique case` takes the enum to
6 bits and roughly 45 members, and the mux that produces `ex_alu_y` grows by two
LUT levels. That mux is on the critical path, and everything downstream of it —
the JALR target, the mispredict compare, the flush — is on it too. **Adding B the
obvious way would cost roughly 1 ns on a path with 0.003 ns of slack, while the
round's other goal is to remove 3.9 ns from it.**

**The resolution, and it is cheap: B does not go in the ALU.** The core already
has the right structure and it is being used for `M` and `Zicsr` today:

    ex_result = is_muldiv ? ex_md_result
              : is_csr    ? ex_csr_rdata
              :             ex_alu_y;

`ex_result` terminates at a **pipeline register**, `ex_mem_q.ex_result`. It is
not on the critical path; `ex_alu_y` is on it only because `ex_jump_target` taps
`ex_alu_y` *before* this mux. So a new `rvntt_bitmanip.sv` with its own operation
enum, joined as one more arm of `ex_result`, adds **one LUT level to a path with
slack and zero levels to the path without it.** The cost is a second 32-bit
operand fan-out and a wider `ex_result` mux; the benefit is that segment 3 does
not grow at all.

**This also removes the reason `alu_op_e` would have to widen**, which matters
beyond timing: every downstream `case` on `alu_op_e` — including
`rvntt_alu.sv`'s `FORMAL` block, which mirrors each operation in a second idiom —
stays exactly as verified.

**And segments 3 and 4 can be deleted outright, which is A26's largest lever.**
`ex_jump_target` taps `ex_alu_y` for one instruction: JALR. Its target is
`(rs1 + imm) & ~1`. **A17 already built an adder that computes exactly
`ex_rs1_fwd + id_ex_q.imm`** — `ex_mem_addr`, for load and store addresses — and
A19 built a second, `ex_pc_target = pc + imm`, for branches and JAL. The comment
at `rvntt_core.sv` where JALR is left on the ALU says its target *"genuinely
depends on a register, which is the whole difference between it and the other two
shapes"* — which is true, and is also exactly why **A17's register-sourced adder
is the right one and was already there**. Nobody noticed, because A17 added it
for loads and A19 added a different one for branches, and JALR is neither.

Routing JALR through `ex_mem_addr` removes segments 3 **and** 4 from the path —
about 2.7 ns of the 12.954 — for **zero new logic**, and it is checkable the way
A17 checked its adder: `a_addr_adder_matches_alu` already asserts
`ex_mem_addr == ex_alu_y` for loads and stores; the JALR form asserts the same
equality under `ctrl.jalr`.

**Sequencing consequence, and it is load-bearing:** this makes A21 (B) and A26
(cycle-neutral Fmax) interact, so they are ordered **B first**. Doing the Fmax
work first and then adding B would put a 1 ns regression on top of a
freshly-tuned path and make A26's measured contribution a fiction. Doing B first,
in its own lane, means A26 measures the machine it will ship on.

### 3.5 Genuinely hard, third: Zkt is a claim about a machine that has a branch predictor

**The ratified list was read, not recalled**, from
`riscv/riscv-crypto`'s `doc/scalar/riscv-crypto-scalar-zkt.adoc`. Three of its
properties are surprising enough that reconstructing the list from memory would
have produced a wrong claim:

**First: `Zkt` excludes division and remainder.** The specification's own words
are *"Cryptographers typically assume division to be variable-time (while
multiplication is constant time)"*. `div`, `divu`, `rem` and `remu` are **not**
on the list; `mul`, `mulh`, `mulhsu` and `mulhu` are. So the premise that
`rvntt_muldiv`'s data-independent divider "pays a second dividend" at `Zkt` is
**wrong** — `Zkt` never asked for it. The divider's data independence is a real
and useful property of this core, it is worth stating, and it is **beyond `Zkt`,
not required by it.** Claiming it as `Zkt` compliance would be claiming credit
under the wrong heading.

**Second: `Zkt` excludes loads, stores and conditional branches**, explicitly:
*"Loads, stores, conditional branches are excluded, along with a set of
instructions that are rarely necessary to process secret data."* `Zicsr` is
excluded too.

**Third: `Zkt`'s bit-manipulation coverage is Zbkb/Zbkx/Zbc, not B.** The listed
instructions are `clmul`, `clmulh`, `xperm4`, `xperm8`, `ror`, `rol`, `rori`,
`andn`, `orn`, `xnor`, `pack`, `packh`, `brev8`, `rev8`, `zip`, `unzip`.
**`sh1add`/`sh2add`/`sh3add` (Zba) are not on it. `clz`/`ctz`/`cpop`/`min`/`max`/
`minu`/`maxu`/`sext.b`/`sext.h`/`zext.h`/`orc.b` (Zbb-only) are not on it.
`bset`/`bclr`/`binv`/`bext` and their immediate forms (Zbs) are not on it.**
So of the 34 instructions A21 adds, the ones `Zkt` constrains are exactly the
Zbb∩Zbkb overlap plus Zbkb's own five — and the rest are simply out of scope.
They are all combinational here anyway, so the *implementation* is uniform; the
*claim* is not, and the claim is what gets written down.

**The two boundaries that must be stated before the claim is made.** A `Zkt`
claim that omits them is a security falsehood, which is worse than no claim:

> **Boundary 1 — `Zkt` is a statement about listed instructions' latency, and
> not about control flow.** It says nothing about which instructions a program
> executes or in what order. Constant-time *code* is the programmer's
> obligation; `Zkt` only guarantees that the primitives it lists do not leak
> through their own timing. Branches are excluded from the list by name.
>
> **Boundary 2 — this core has a branch predictor, and it introduces
> data-dependent timing that `Zkt` does not cover and this core does not
> remove.** A19's 256-entry BTB, its 2-bit counters and its 8-entry RAS are
> **architecturally invisible state that persists across whatever runs on the
> core.** There is no flush, no partition and no privilege boundary — there is
> only machine mode. So: a mispredict costs 2 cycles and whether it occurs
> depends on branch history, which depends on data; and BTB and counter state
> **survive from one program to the next**, which is the standard precondition
> for a branch-predictor side channel. A `Zkt`-conformant core is not a
> constant-time machine, and this one specifically is not.

**Boundary 2 is not a defect being confessed — it is what `Zkt` is for.** The
extension exists precisely because "constant-time" is not a property a whole
machine has; it is a property individual instructions can be guaranteed to have,
so that code written to avoid secret-dependent control flow can rely on its
primitives. Saying so is the difference between a useful claim and a misleading
one.

**The proof obligation, and the vacuity risk.** On `rvntt_muldiv` the property is
close to a single formal statement: EX occupancy depends only on the opcode. The
danger is that it passes without constraining anything — the same failure as
A11's first riscv-formal run, which reported 43/43 over a broken adder. **A30's
done-when therefore requires the proof to FAIL under an injected fault**: give
the multiplier a data-dependent early-out and confirm the property catches it.
A `Zkt` proof that has never been observed to fail is a security claim resting on
nothing.

### 3.6 The moving baselines, stated before they are measured

`MODS_A` amended §B6 and §10 M2/M3 once. **This round amends them again, and the
amendment is written here, before the measurement, so it cannot later be
presented as a discovery.**

**Two changes in this round make the software NTT faster**, and the software NTT
is the denominator of §10 M2's kernel speedup and a term in §10 M3's Amdahl
breakdown:

1. **B and Zbkb.** Effect on the NTT itself: **small** — the reference NTT is
   multiply-and-add over 16-bit coefficients and has little for Zbb to do. Effect
   on Keccak: **large**, which is §10 M3's other term, not §10 M2's.
2. **A 2-cycle multiply (A25).** Effect on the NTT: **large.** A18 measured
   multi-cycle EX stalls at ~20% of the RV32IM software-NTT region, and this
   halves the stall term.

**The pre-committed consequence, numbered so it can be checked against later:**

> **P1. The reported Tier-1 and Tier-2 speedup ratios in §10 M2 will get
> SMALLER**, because their denominator — the software NTT — gets faster while
> their numerator does not change. **This is the correct direction.** A speedup
> measured against a deliberately weak baseline is the thing §10 M2's own text
> warns about: *"If you benchmark against a naive `-O0` triple-loop, your speedup
> number is meaningless and anyone who knows the field will see it
> immediately."* A faster baseline is a more honest number, not a worse result.
>
> **P2. §10 M3's application-level speedup will get smaller too, and by more than
> P1**, because B accelerates Keccak — the dominant term — while the NTT
> coprocessor does not touch it. Amdahl's denominator shrinks where the
> accelerator has no effect.
>
> **P3. Dhrystone and CoreMark will improve, and the two will not agree.** As at
> A16: CoreMark is multiply- and bit-manipulation-heavy, Dhrystone is
> string- and branch-heavy. If they move together by similar amounts, something
> is wrong with the measurement.
>
> **P4. IPC may FALL while performance rises**, and this must not be reported as
> a regression. B replaces instruction sequences with single instructions, so
> retired-instruction count drops faster than cycles do. `MODS_A` §3.4's rule
> applies unchanged: per-MHz ratios and IPC measure different things than
> absolute throughput, and an improvement in one may show as a decline in
> another.

**The earlier records are kept, not replaced** — as M7's RV32I figures were kept
when M7.1 replaced them, and as M7.1's were kept at M7.2. After this round the
record is four deep: `docs/a13-benchmarks.md` (RV32I),
`docs/a16-benchmarks.md` (RV32IM), `docs/a19-benchmarks.md` (RV32IM + predictor)
and this round's (RV32IM + B + Zicond + 2-cycle multiply). **The dual software
NTT baseline is measured at both M7.3 and M7.4** — once after the ISA additions
and once after the multiplier change — because those are two different machines
and a single number would attribute the whole change to whichever step happened
to be reported.

### 3.7 Two budgets, not conflicts

**The memory budget — re-examined under a 110 MHz target and NOT changed.**
A17 measured 64 KB (16 tiles) as **≥ +3.5 MHz** against 128 KB's 86.490 MHz and
declined to adopt it, on the grounds that ML-KEM-768's keys, ciphertext and
Keccak state, the coefficient arrays, and above all **the Tier-2 coprocessor's
own buffers — which do not exist yet** — cannot be sized against an array already
given away. A 110 MHz goal is a reason to re-open that, so it was re-opened, and
the answer is the same: **nothing about this round changes what Tier 2 will
need**, and §3.3 has just concluded that Tier 1's requirements are *tighter* than
previously assumed, not looser. Spending memory now to buy clock, in a round
whose own analysis says the coprocessor's demands are less well understood than
they looked, is the wrong order. **There is no third OVERRIDES row against
§1.4.** If the levers in A26 and A27 fall short of target and this becomes the
difference, it is re-opened again — with the row written, per `MODS_A` A17's
rule.

**The entropy budget — the one genuinely new resource question.** A ring
oscillator is a combinational loop, which every tool in this flow is built to
refuse: Vivado will optimise it away without `DONT_TOUCH`/`KEEP_HIERARCHY`, and
will error on the loop without an explicit `set_disable_timing` /
`create_clock`-free exclusion. Verilator will not simulate it meaningfully at
all, and Yosys/SymbiYosys cannot — **so the entropy source is the first module in
this project that cannot be verified by the project's own primary methods.**
That is not a reason to skip it; it is a reason to structure it so that
everything *except* the oscillator is verifiable: the health tests, the
`seed` CSR's return-code state machine, and the conditioning are all ordinary
synchronous logic and get ordinary formal and simulation coverage, driven by a
**stub source** the testbench can make stuck, biased or ideal at will. §A29
specifies exactly that split, and it is the reason the step's done-when is about
the health tests firing rather than about the oscillator running.

## 4. Track A additions

Eleven steps, in three phases. The phase boundaries are the milestones in §6, and
each phase is coherent, tagged and defensible on its own if everything after it
is abandoned.

**Sequencing, and why it is this way.** Instrument before optimising, so A20's
counters come first — A18's lesson, and A5's before it. Cheap decoder-only
additions before expensive datapath ones, so B and Zicond precede the multiplier
and the floorplan. **Fmax work that changes cycle counts is kept strictly
separate from Fmax work that does not**: A25 changes cycles and has its own
before/after; A26 and A27 change none and are held to A17's bit-identical
standard. B lands before the Fmax work for the reason in §3.4 — tuning a path and
then widening it measures nothing. And **Zkt is last**, because it is a proof
*about* everything else and has to be re-proved after every functional addition.

### Phase 1 — the ISA round (M7.3)

### A20 — Hardware performance counters (`Zihpm`)

**Instrument before optimising.** Every attribution number in this project is a
simulation result validated against two hardware counters (§1). This step makes
the terms first-hand.

**Build:** `mhpmcounter3–31` and `mhpmcounter3h–31h` as 64-bit counters, with
`mhpmevent3–31` selectors and `mcountinhibit`. Not all 29 need to be real —
the specification permits a subset, with unimplemented counters **read-only
zero** — and implementing 29 × 64 bits of counter on a design whose Fmax is the
point of the round would be self-defeating. **Implement 6 real counters** and
tie the rest to zero, with the event set chosen to close A18's identity:

| Event | Why it is one of the six |
|---|---|
| branch mispredicts | A19's payoff, measured on the board for the first time |
| BTB hits | separates "predicted correctly" from "not predicted at all" |
| load-use interlock cycles | A18 identity term |
| multi-cycle EX stall cycles | A18 identity term; A25 changes it directly |
| taken control transfers retired | the denominator for a mispredict *rate* |
| instruction-fetch redirects | closes the identity against `mcycle`/`minstret` |

**The check that makes it trustworthy is the one A18 used**, and it is now
checkable on hardware rather than only in simulation:

    mcycle = minstret + load_use + muldiv_stalls + 2 × redirects

**Verify:** `mcountinhibit` actually inhibits (write it, run a loop, confirm the
counter does not move); the counters are WARL and writable; a 32-bit overflow
carries into the `h` half. **Fault-inject:** miscount one event by one per
occurrence and confirm the identity's residual becomes nonzero — a counter set
that cannot be shown to break the identity is not validating it.
**Cross-validate against A18**: run both instruments over the same delimited
region and require agreement **to the count**, not to within a tolerance. That is
the whole point of having built the simulation instrument first.

**Realistic expectation:** 6 × 64-bit counters plus selectors is roughly 400 FFs
and 250 LUTs — the counters themselves are not on any critical path, but the
**event wires are**, because several of them are derived from `ex_mispredict` and
`ex_redirect`, which §3.4 shows are on it. Expect a small Fmax cost, and measure
it rather than assuming it is zero.

**Stop rule:** if the event wiring costs more than ~1 MHz, register the events
one stage later — a counter does not care when it is incremented as long as it is
incremented exactly once. **Do not** solve it by dropping counters; the whole
step is the measurement.

**Done when:** the six counters agree with A18's instrument to the count on both
benchmarks, the identity closes with residual **exactly zero on hardware**,
`mcountinhibit` is shown to inhibit, and the miscount fault-injection is shown to
break the residual.

### A21 — B (Zba + Zbb + Zbs) and Zbkb, in a separate result lane

**The largest step in the round by instruction count and the cheapest by
risk**, because everything it adds is combinational, single-cycle, externally
referenced and already supported by every checker this project uses (§3.1).

**Build:** `rvntt_bitmanip.sv` — 34 instructions: Zba's 3, Zbb's 18, Zbs's 8 and
Zbkb's 5 beyond the Zbb overlap. **It is a separate module with its own operation
enum and its own result, joined as one more arm of `ex_result`, and
`rvntt_alu.sv` is not touched.** §3.4 is the reason and it is not negotiable:
folding these into the ALU puts two LUT levels on the critical path in the round
whose other goal is to shorten it.

Structurally the unit is four groups sharing operands: a shift-and-rotate group
(`rol`, `ror`, `rori`) that reuses the existing barrel-shifter idiom; a
count/compare group (`clz`, `ctz`, `cpop`, `min`, `max`, `minu`, `maxu`); a
byte/bit-permute group (`rev8`, `orc.b`, `brev8`, `zip`, `unzip`, `pack`,
`packh`, `sext.b`, `sext.h`, `zext.h`); and a single-bit group (`bset`, `bclr`,
`binv`, `bext` and immediate forms) which is a shifter plus a mask. `sh1add`/
`sh2add`/`sh3add` are a shift-by-constant into the existing adder and are the one
group with a plausible claim to belong in the ALU — **put them in the bitmanip
unit anyway**, so that the rule "B is not in the ALU" has no exceptions to
remember.

**`misa` bit 1 is NOT set, and §2's note says why** — riscv-config 3.18.3
cannot express the `B` letter, and arch-test selects the suites from the ISA
string rather than from `misa`, so asserting it would cost the whole compliance
run and buy nothing.

**Verify — and this is the step where the verification surface is paid:**

1. **RISCOF, the B suite**, with `clmul`, `clmulh` and `clmulr` **excluded by
   name** and the exclusion recorded the way M5 records `pmp` (§3.1). Report
   *29/29 ratified-B tests, 3 `Zbc` tests excluded*, never "32/32".
2. **RISCOF, the Zbkb subset of the `K` suite** — `pack`, `packh`, `brev8_32`,
   `zip`, `unzip`. The rest of `K` is AES/SHA/SM3/SM4 and is not implemented;
   exclude by name and record that boundary too.
3. **riscv-formal**, with the check list composed as the union of `rv32im`,
   `rv32ib` and `rv32iZbkb` (§3.1 — no `isa_rv32imb` bundle exists). **Depth
   stays 14**; every instruction here is combinational and adds no window.
4. **Cosim**: 1000 random programs with B and Zbkb at high density, against
   Spike, byte-identical.
5. **A3's decoder equivalence sweep extended to the new encodings** — 10⁶ random
   words, RTL decoder against `model/rv32i_ref.py`, with the strict
   reserved-field rule intact. This is the check that a lax decoder fails and it
   is the reason §3.1 enumerates the newly-legal `funct7` values instead of
   describing them.
6. **Mutations.** At minimum: a `rori` that rotates the wrong way, a `clz` that
   is off by one on a zero input (the specification says 32, and off-by-one here
   is the classic), a `sext.b` that zero-extends, a `bext` that reads the wrong
   bit, and a `zip`/`unzip` pair that are each other's inverse but both wrong.
   Each must be caught and by which test recorded.

**Realistic expectation:** +600 to +900 LUTs — the count/permute groups are the
expensive ones, and `zip`/`unzip`/`brev8` are pure wiring. **Fmax cost near zero
by construction**, because the unit is off the critical path; if it is not near
zero, the lane separation was not achieved and that is the thing to fix, not the
Fmax. Keccak speedup on the ML-KEM profile is the point of the step and is
measured at A23, not asserted here.

**Stop rule:** if `rvntt_bitmanip` lands on the critical path, stop and fix the
lane before continuing — that is a structural failure of the step's central
decision, and carrying it forward would corrupt A26's and A27's before/after.

**Done when:** all six verification items above pass with the two exclusions
recorded by name, `misa` reads `0x40001100` with the `B`-bit boundary recorded, `rvntt_isa.yaml`'s reset value and
bitmask agree with the hardware, and `rvntt_alu.sv` is untouched in the diff.

### A22 — Zicond, and re-measuring what the predictor is worth

**Two instructions, `czero.eqz` and `czero.nez`**, one `funct7`, no new operand
paths — the cheapest ISA addition in the project. They are specified as their own
step anyway, because of what they do to a *measurement* rather than what they
cost to build.

**Build:** decode `funct7 = 0000111` in `OP`, `funct3 = 101` (`czero.eqz`) and
`111` (`czero.nez`). The result is `rs2 == 0 ? 0 : rs1` and its complement —
a 32-bit AND with a replicated zero-detect. Put it in the bitmanip lane, for the
same reason as everything in A21.

**The measurement-ordering trap, and the choice made.** Zicond converts short
if-then-else into straight-line code, so it **removes branches**, so it changes
what A19's predictor is worth. The prompt's two options were to sequence it
before the predictor's numbers are final, or to re-run A18's instrument
afterwards and report the delta.

**The choice is to re-measure afterwards, and the reason is that the first option
no longer exists.** M7.2 is complete, committed, tagged `m7.2-bpred-77mhz` and
pushed. Reopening it to re-sequence a later extension would violate the
project's own rule — the same rule that kept M7 from being retagged when M7.1
superseded its numbers. So:

**A22's own done-when includes re-running A18's instrument and A20's counters
over both benchmarks and reporting, separately:** total branches retired before
and after; mispredicts before and after; and **the predictor's payoff recomputed
on the post-Zicond branch stream**. The expected shape is that Zicond removes
*easy, well-predicted* branches — a hammock around a single instruction is almost
always correctly predicted — so **the mispredict rate may go UP while the
mispredict count goes DOWN.** Report both; either alone is misleading.

> **REVISED AFTER MEASURING, AND THE REVISION IS THE POINT OF THE STEP.** Two
> builds are not enough. `Zbb`'s `min`/`max` are themselves if-conversion
> instructions and `Zba`'s `sh*add` replace multiplies, so an `rv32im` versus
> `rv32im+B+Zicond` comparison **cannot separate what Zicond did from what B
> did** — and run that way, Dhrystone appears to lose 2,000 branches and 3,999
> mispredicts together, which reads as Zicond removing a fifth of the
> mispredicts. **A third build, B without Zicond, says B removed all 3,999 and
> Zicond removed none.**
>
> So this step's done-when now requires **three** points — `rv32im`,
> `rv32im+B`, `rv32im+B+Zicond` — and `build_bench_image.py` grew an
> `rv32imzb` arch that exists for no other purpose. The predicted shape was
> half right: the count did not move at all and the rate rose 0.11 points
> purely because the denominator shrank. See `docs/a22-zicond.md`.

**Verify:** RISCOF's `Zicond` suite, both tests. Cosim against Spike, whose
`czero_eqz.h`/`czero_nez.h` already exist. `model/bpred.py` re-run against the
new stream and required to still match the RTL redirect count **to the unit**,
which is M7.2's second closure and is the check that Zicond has not perturbed
anything it should not have. **Fault-inject:** swap `eqz` and `nez` — cosim must
catch it; and make `czero` return `rs2` instead of zero.

**Realistic expectation:** under 50 LUTs. Dhrystone barely moves; CoreMark and
the ML-KEM rejection-sampling loop are where conditional-select code lives.
**Do not expect much.** The step's value is that a cheap, ratified,
constant-time-friendly primitive is present and measured, not that it is fast.

**Stop rule:** none needed — if it does not help, it still costs almost nothing
and it is on `Zkt`-adjacent ground (§3.5 notes `Zicond` is *not* on the ratified
`Zkt` list, which is itself worth recording).

**Done when:** both RISCOF tests pass, cosim passes, the predictor's payoff is
re-reported on the post-Zicond stream **from three builds, so B's contribution
and Zicond's are separated**, with count and rate shown separately, and
`model/bpred.py` still matches RTL redirects to the unit in every build.

### Phase 2 — performance (M7.4)

### A24 — The Tier-1 timing probe: what Fmax is Track B allowed to inherit?

**This is the cheapest step in the round and the one with the largest
consequence, and it is deliberately first in the phase.** §3.3 concluded that
Tier-1 `Xkntt` cannot be in a separate clock domain, so the core's Fmax is a hard
floor for the Tier-1 butterfly. **Committing the core to a number the butterfly
cannot meet is the §9 failure mode fired at M15, two tracks away from its
cause.** So the evidence is taken before the commitment, not after.

**Build:** a *representative* Tier-1 datapath — not the real one, which is §B1's
and §B2's job. It needs to be pessimistic in the right way: a 16×16 modular
multiply with Montgomery reduction against `q = 3329`, feeding a
conditional add/subtract, pipelined to the depth `docs/isa-spec.md`'s 4-cycle
`kmm` and 5-cycle `kbfct` allow. That is enough to find the frequency at which
the reduction's carry chain stops closing, which is the thing being asked.

**Measure:** out-of-context synthesis and implementation with
`fpga/scripts/synth_ooc.sh`, on the same part and speed grade, at successively
tighter constraints. **Report its Fmax, its DSP count and its LUT count.**

**Read `CLAUDE.md`'s warning about this script before trusting its output.**
A14's first version reported `DSP=0` over a netlist containing four DSP48E1s
because it filtered on guessed `PRIMITIVE_TYPE` group names. **A checking script
that guesses is worse than none**, and this step's entire value is its number.
Confirm the DSP count against the synthesis log independently before using it.

**Realistic expectation:** a DSP48E1 with its internal pipeline registers runs
far above 110 MHz; the risk is not the multiply, it is the Montgomery reduction's
conditional subtract and the fabric around it. Expect **comfortably above
110 MHz** for a pipelined single butterfly, and treat any result below 120 MHz as
a finding that changes A26's and A27's target.

**Stop rule — and this is the round's Fmax ceiling, stated in advance:**
**do not adopt a core Fmax that this probe says the Tier-1 butterfly cannot
meet with at least 10% margin.** If the probe returns 105 MHz, the core's target
becomes ~95 MHz and the 110 MHz goal is reported as *bounded by Track B*, not as
missed. Chasing core clock past the point where the coprocessor can follow makes
the integrated design slower, not faster, because §9 will have to re-time the
whole thing downward.

**Done when:** the probe's Fmax, DSP and LUT numbers are recorded with the DSP
count independently confirmed, and A26/A27's target frequency is stated as a
number derived from it.

### A25 — A two-cycle multiply

**The first step in this round that changes cycle counts**, and it is kept in its
own step with its own before/after for exactly that reason.

**Build:** A14's `rvntt_muldiv.sv` computes a 33×33 product on 4 DSP48E1 with
**4 cycles** of EX occupancy. The DSP48E1 has internal `AREG`/`BREG`/`MREG`/
`PREG` pipeline registers; using them properly is the difference between a
multiplier that closes at 400 MHz and one that closes at 90. Target **2 cycles**.
The divider is untouched — it stays at 34 cycles and stays data-independent by
construction, which §3.5 establishes is a property beyond `Zkt` rather than one
`Zkt` requires.

**This helps the Fmax goal rather than fighting it**, which is unusual for a
latency reduction and is why it is in this phase: moving multiplication into DSP
internal registers takes fabric logic *off* the design, and A14's own note says
neither the multiplier nor the divider is on the critical path today. Confirm
that is still true afterwards rather than assuming it survived.

**Verify:** all 8 `rv32um` tests; RISCOF's `M` suite; `formal_muldiv`'s
standalone proof re-run at whatever depth 2 cycles now needs — **lower than 37,
so this gets easier**; riscv-formal at depth 14 with the arithmetic still
abstracted per M6's recorded boundary; cosim with `M` at high hazard density.
**`tb/cosim/cycle_model.py`'s multi-cycle term changes from 3 to 1** and the
model must still predict the RTL's span exactly — that is the check that the
latency really is 2 and not 3-with-a-lucky-forwarding-case.
**Fault-inject:** make the result available one cycle early and confirm cosim's
span check fails; a latency change that no timing check notices means the model
was not actually watching.

**Realistic expectation:** A18 measured multi-cycle EX stalls at ~20% of the
RV32IM software-NTT region, so halving `MUL`'s latency should remove roughly
**10% of that region's cycles**. Dhrystone's IPC should *rise* — A16 measured
Dhrystone giving back almost all of `M`'s instruction-count win in 4-cycle `MUL`
stalls, and this is the direct reversal of that. CoreMark less so. **§3.6 P1
applies: this makes the §10 M2 denominator smaller.**

**Stop rule:** if 2 cycles cannot be reached without a data-dependent path,
**stay at 3** — a variable-latency multiply would break `Zkt` (§3.5, where `mul`
*is* on the ratified list) and A30 would then have to report a failure caused by
this step. Latency is worth less than the guarantee.

**Done when:** `rv32um`, RISCOF `M`, `formal_muldiv`, riscv-formal and cosim all
pass; the cycle model's changed term still predicts spans exactly; the
fault-injection is shown to be caught; and the multi-cycle EX stall term is
re-measured with A20's counters on hardware.

### A26 — Fmax, part 1: the levers that change no cycles

**Held to A17's standard: not one cycle count may change.** Dhrystone and
CoreMark on this SoC are deterministic to the cycle, so before and after must be
**identical except for the clock** — same `dhry_cycles`, same `cm_cycles`, same
`minstret`, same CRCs. A lever that moves a cycle count is a microarchitecture
change wearing a timing change's clothes and belongs in its own step.

**Build, in this order, and measure each one separately** — bundling makes the
result unattributable, which is the mistake A17 was careful not to make:

1. **JALR onto A17's address adder.** §3.4: `ex_jump_target` taps `ex_alu_y` for
   JALR alone, and `ex_mem_addr = ex_rs1_fwd + id_ex_q.imm` is already the same
   sum. Removes the **ALU result mux and the JALR target form** — segments 3 and
   4, about **2.7 ns of 12.954** — for zero new logic. Expect the largest single
   contribution in the round. Check it the way A17 checked its own adder:
   assert `ex_mem_addr == ex_alu_y` under `ctrl.jalr`, so the claim is proved
   rather than reasoned.
2. **Precompute the forwarding-mux selects in ID.** Segment 1 is 3.35 ns and
   starts at a `rd_addr` comparator. The comparison for an instruction in EX is
   against producers in MEM and WB; computed a stage earlier it is against
   producers in EX and MEM, which is the same decision reached one cycle sooner.
   **This is the delicate one**: the ID-stage computation must be correct across
   a load-use interlock, a multi-cycle EX stall and a flush, and getting any of
   the three wrong produces a forwarding bug — the exact class riscv-formal found
   at A11. Expect **1.0–1.5 ns**. Run the full formal set, not a subset.
3. **Shorten the mispredict comparison.** Segment 5 is a 30-bit equality on
   `pred_target` against `ex_jump_target`, 2.03 ns of CARRY4. It only has to be
   *correct*, not *fast*, if the redirect it drives can tolerate being registered
   — but it cannot, because A19 already established that a late redirect puts the
   ALU back in the fetch cone. So the lever is narrowing the comparison, not
   delaying it: compare a **hash** of the target rather than the target, accept
   the vanishingly rare false match as a *correct-but-slower* prediction — it
   redirects to the right place regardless, because the redirect target is
   `ex_jump_target`, not the prediction. **This costs no correctness and may cost
   a few cycles**; if it costs any, it moves to A27's phase or is dropped. Verify
   with `model/bpred.py`, which must still match RTL redirects to the unit.
4. **Re-check `MAX_FANOUT`.** A17 tried this on `ex_trap` and it did nothing.
   Segment 6 crosses a fanout-131 net (`if_id_q[valid]`) and segment 1 a
   fanout-66. **A17's result says aim it by measurement and expect little**; it
   is listed because it is nearly free, not because it is promising.

**Verify:** the full regression, riscv-formal at depth 14, RISCOF, cosim, and
the benchmark cycle counts bit-identical to A23's. Lever 2 touches the forwarding
network, which is the best-covered logic in the core after the trap path; run
`formal_forward`, `formal_hazard` and the full riscv-formal set, and
**fault-inject the ID-stage select**: make it miss the load-use case, and make it
forward from a flushed instruction. Both must be caught by something other than
timing.

**Realistic expectation:** levers 1 and 2 together remove ~3.9 ns of 12.954,
which would be **~110 MHz** if nothing else became critical. **Something else
will become critical** — that is exactly what happened at A17, where fixing the
store path promoted decode-and-operand-select to the longest thing in the design.
So expect **95–110 MHz**, with 110 reachable only if the newly-exposed path is
also short. Report the new critical path by name either way; that is the input to
whether A27 has anything to work on.

**Stop rule:** any lever that moves a cycle count is rejected and re-filed.
A26 does not proceed past the frequency A24's probe permits.

### A27 — Fmax, part 2: floorplanning, which has never been tried

**67.6% of the critical path is route delay at 3.5% utilisation.** That is not
congestion — there is nothing to congest. It is the placer spreading a
19-level chain across a die it has no reason to keep it on one corner of.
**No pblock has ever been tried on this design.**

**Build:** no RTL change at all, which makes this the only step in the round that
is cycle-neutral by construction rather than by measurement.

1. **A pblock around `u_core`**, sized with modest slack over its ~3500 LUTs and
   placed adjacent to the BRAM column the 32 tiles occupy. The single most likely
   win, because the core-to-BRAM crossing and the intra-core chain are pulled
   together instead of competing.
2. **A tighter nested pblock** around the EX-stage cluster the critical path
   lives in — forwarding network, adders, mispredict compare — if step 1 helps.
3. **The BRAM tiles' own placement.** A17's memory experiment showed 16 tiles
   beats 32 by ≥3.5 MHz, and §3.7 declines to cut memory. **Constraining where
   the 32 tiles go is the same lever without the cost**, and it has not been
   tried.

**Verify:** cycle counts bit-identical — trivially true, since no RTL changed,
and therefore **the check is that the bitstream still works on hardware**, not
that the counts match. A pblock that is too tight fails to place; one that is
subtly wrong routes and runs. Run the three-pass hardware benchmark.

**Realistic expectation:** genuinely uncertain, which is why it is its own step
and last. Route-dominated paths at low utilisation are the case floorplanning is
*for*, so **1–8 MHz is plausible and a larger number would not be shocking**;
equally, Vivado's placer may already be doing the sensible thing and the answer
may be zero. **Both outcomes are results.** A17's lever 2 and lever 3 are the
precedent in each direction: one aimed by the report and worth nothing, one free
and worth exactly the measurement spread.

**Stop rule — and this is where the round's Fmax work ends regardless of the
number reached.** If A26 and A27 together land below 110 MHz having worked
through the levers above, **that is the result, and it is reported as one.**
Further Fmax work past this point means pipeline surgery — splitting EX,
re-timing the register file, a deeper fetch — every one of which changes cycle
counts, changes the hazard structure, invalidates A18's identity and A19's
predictor model, and makes §8 I1's Tier-1 integration harder against a moving
target. **That is explicitly out of scope**, and the reason is the one that
governs the whole phase: the coprocessor is what this project is for, and a core
that is 15% faster and harder to integrate is a net loss.

**Done when:** each pblock configuration's Fmax is reported separately, the
adopted configuration's bitstream is validated on hardware over three passes, and
the final critical path is named.

### Phase 3 — the cryptographic guarantees (M7.5)

### A29 — `Zkr`: the `seed` CSR and an entropy source

**The highest-risk item in the round and the easiest to over-claim.** The CSR is
easy. The source is not, and the honest deliverable is a working source with its
mandatory health tests and an explicit statement of what has *not* been done.

**Build, in three separable pieces**, because §3.7 established that only one of
them can be verified by this project's normal methods:

1. **`rvntt_entropy.sv` — the noise source.** A ring oscillator: an odd-length
   inverter chain, sampled by the system clock, with the jitter between the two
   as the entropy. On Artix-7 this needs `(* DONT_TOUCH = "TRUE" *)` and
   `(* KEEP_HIERARCHY = "YES" *)` or synthesis will constant-fold the loop away,
   and an explicit timing exclusion (`set_disable_timing` on the closing arc, and
   the loop declared to `report_timing` as a false path) or implementation will
   error on the combinational loop. **Several independent oscillators of
   different lengths, XORed**, rather than one — a single ring locks to its
   sampling clock and produces a periodic sequence that looks like entropy to a
   casual test and is not.
2. **`rvntt_entropy_health.sv` — the two mandatory SP 800-90B health tests.**
   The **repetition count test** (a run of identical samples longer than the
   cutoff is a hard failure) and the **adaptive proportion test** (in a window of
   1024 samples, a count of any one value above the cutoff is a failure). These
   are ordinary synchronous logic, they are where the security value is, and
   **they are fully verifiable.**
3. **The `seed` CSR (`0x015`) and its state machine.** `seed` is **not a normal
   CSR**: it is read-write-only — a `csrrw`-class access with `rd = x0` is the
   read — and it returns a two-bit status in `[31:30]`: `BIST`, `WAIT`, `ES16`
   (16 bits of entropy in `[15:0]`) or `DEAD`. **`DEAD` is latching**: once a
   health test fails, the source stays dead until reset. Software polls for
   `ES16`.

**The KAT path must not touch any of this, and both paths must coexist.** ML-KEM
KATs need deterministic seeds — that is what makes them KATs. **The entropy
source must not be wired into the KAT path at all**, and the existing KATs must
still pass unchanged. This is the failure mode that is silent in both directions:
a KAT that passes because the source was bypassed proves nothing about the
source, and a keygen that is deterministic in the field is a catastrophic bug
that no KAT can ever catch. Keep them structurally separate — a build-time
selection between "seeds from `seed`" and "seeds from the KAT vector", with the
KAT build unable to reach the CSR — rather than a runtime flag.

**Verify — and this is the step where fault injection matters more than usual:**

- **The health tests must be shown to FIRE.** Drive the stub source stuck-at-0,
  stuck-at-1, and with a biased pattern (e.g. 7:1), and confirm the repetition
  and adaptive tests each trigger and that `seed` latches `DEAD`. **A health test
  that has never been observed to fire is not a health test.** Prove it formally
  too: `tb/formal/rvntt_entropy_health/` — a stuck input implies `DEAD` within
  the cutoff.
- The `seed` state machine: `BIST` → `WAIT`/`ES16`, `DEAD` is latching and
  survives further reads, a read returns fresh bits each time (two consecutive
  `ES16` reads must not return the same value from the same buffer), and
  a **non-machine-mode access traps** — vacuously true here since only machine
  mode exists, and worth an assertion anyway so it stays true if that changes.
- The existing ML-KEM KATs still pass, and the KAT build is shown to contain no
  reference to the CSR.

**What must be written down, in this document and in the write-up:**

> **No SP 800-90B statistical validation campaign has been run on this entropy
> source, and it is therefore UNCERTIFIED.** What exists is a noise source of a
> standard construction and the two health tests SP 800-90B makes mandatory,
> shown by fault injection to detect a stuck and a biased source. What does not
> exist is the entropy-rate estimation, the restart tests, or the IID/non-IID
> track that certification requires — none of which is a simulation exercise and
> all of which need a large sample captured from the physical device.
> **`ES16` is returned because the CSR's interface requires a status code, and
> its specified meaning is entropy meeting SP 800-90B. This implementation does
> not establish that.** Anyone using this for real key material should treat the
> source as unvalidated.

**Realistic expectation:** ~200 LUTs and ~150 FFs, none of it on the critical
path — the source runs asynchronously and the CSR read is a normal CSR path.
**The ring oscillator will fight the tools**, and the first two attempts will
probably be optimised away or fail implementation; that is the expected cost of
the step, not a sign it is going wrong.

**Stop rule:** if the oscillator cannot be made to survive synthesis *and* be
shown to produce non-periodic output, **ship the health tests, the CSR and the
state machine against a clearly-labelled deterministic stub, and record that the
noise source is absent.** A recorded gap is worth more than an unverifiable
claim — M5 and M6 both do exactly this. What must not happen is a source that is
present, unverified and quietly assumed to work.

**Done when:** the health tests are shown by fault injection to fire on stuck and
biased sources and to latch `DEAD`; the `seed` state machine passes its directed
tests; the ML-KEM KATs pass unchanged with the entropy path structurally absent
from that build; and the uncertified statement above is in the write-up.

### A30 — `Zkt`: the data-independent-latency proof

**Last, deliberately.** It is a proof *about* everything else in the round and
must be re-proved after every functional addition — so it is proved once, at the
end, against the machine that ships.

**Build:** nothing, if the rest of the round was built correctly. That is the
point of doing it last rather than the reason to skip it.

**The claim, scoped from the ratified specification** (§3.5 — read, not
recalled). This core implements, from `Zkt`'s list: the RV32I base arithmetic,
logical and shift instructions; `mul`, `mulh`, `mulhsu`, `mulhu`; and from the
bit-manipulation groups, `ror`, `rol`, `rori`, `andn`, `orn`, `xnor`, `pack`,
`packh`, `brev8`, `rev8`, `zip`, `unzip`. It does **not** implement `clmul`,
`clmulh`, `xperm4`, `xperm8`, the RVK crypto instructions, or `C` — so those
parts of the list are vacuous here and **must be reported as not-implemented
rather than as passing.**

**Prove:** that each implemented listed instruction's EX occupancy depends only
on its opcode. For everything except `rvntt_muldiv` this is immediate — the
instruction is combinational and occupies EX for exactly one cycle — and the
property is that `ex_stall` is deasserted for it regardless of operand values.
On `rvntt_muldiv` it is close to a single formal property:

    two executions of the same opcode with ARBITRARY DIFFERENT operands
    produce the same busy-cycle count

`MODS_A`'s own lesson from A15's divider applies directly and should save the
same fifteen minutes: **when a property asks a solver to relate two circuits that
compute the same arithmetic, state the invariant they both maintain rather than
the conclusion they both reach.** Here the invariant is that the sequencer's
state is a function of the opcode and the cycle index alone — never of the
operands — which is a property of one circuit and needs no second copy.

**The vacuity check is the done-when, not an extra.** §3.5: **the proof must be
observed to FAIL under an injected fault.** Give the multiplier a data-dependent
early-out — return in 1 cycle when either operand is zero, which is the
optimisation a real design would actually be tempted by — and confirm the
property catches it. Then remove it. **A `Zkt` proof that has never failed is a
security claim resting on nothing**, and this project has twice shipped a
checker that reported success over a broken design.

**The two boundaries go in the write-up, verbatim from §3.5**, and the claim is
not made without them: `Zkt` covers listed instructions' latency and **not**
control flow; and this core's branch predictor introduces data-dependent timing
through mispredicts and through BTB and counter state that **persists across
whatever runs on the core**, with no flush and no partition. **A `Zkt` claim that
omits them is a security falsehood, which is worse than no claim.**

**Also record what `Zkt` does not ask for and this core provides anyway:** the
divider is data-independent by construction. §3.5 establishes `Zkt` excludes
`div`/`rem` explicitly, so this is a property beyond the extension — worth
stating, worth *not* filing under `Zkt` compliance.

**Realistic expectation:** the proof converges quickly, because the invariant
form is shallow and `rvntt_muldiv`'s sequencer is small. The work is in the
enumeration and the write-up, not the solver.

**Stop rule:** if any listed instruction turns out to have data-dependent
occupancy and cannot be fixed cheaply, **do not implement `Zkt`** — report the
instruction and the reason. A partial `Zkt` is not a thing; the extension is a
guarantee and a guarantee with an exception is not one.

**Done when:** the property is proved for every implemented listed instruction;
the injected early-out is shown to break it; the not-implemented portions of the
list are reported as such; both boundaries are written; and `misa` and the
`--isa` strings are updated together with the RISCOF YAML.

## 6. Milestone additions

Added to §12's sequence. `RISC-V_NTT.txt` is not edited and `RISC-V_NTT_MODS_A.txt`
is not edited; these live here.

- [ ] **M7.3** — **The ISA round: B, Zicond and hardware counters.**
  *Satisfied by A20, A21, A22 and A23.* The six `Zihpm` counters agree with
  A18's simulation instrument **to the count** on both benchmarks, and the
  `cycles = retired + load-use + multi-cycle EX + 2 × redirects` identity closes
  on hardware to **a small constant residual whose magnitude is accounted for by
  the counter-read sequence**.

  > **REVISED AFTER MEASURING: "exactly zero on hardware" was unachievable as
  > written, and the reason is structural rather than a shortfall.** A18's
  > instrument samples every counter at one instant, so its residual is exactly
  > zero and that is a real result. **Software cannot do that.** On hardware each
  > of `mcycle`, `minstret` and the six HPM counters is read by its own
  > instruction, so the four windows are *nested*, not identical, and the
  > residual is whatever the nesting makes it — the reads themselves execute
  > inside some windows and outside others.
  >
  > A23 measured −38 on Dhrystone and −168 on CoreMark, and the difference
  > between them was the finding: the two regions' glue nested the reads in
  > **different orders**, and CoreMark's put both `bench_hpm_read()` calls
  > inside the retired-instruction window. Aligning the order (HPM outermost,
  > then `minstret`, then `mcycle`) makes the residual a property of the read
  > sequence rather than of which benchmark is being looked at.
  >
  > So the requirement becomes: **the residual is small, of a sign and magnitude
  > the read ordering predicts, and constant rather than proportional** — the
  > same distinction A20 established for the software-read overhead, and for the
  > same reason. Residual exactly zero remains the standard for A18's
  > simulation instrument, where it is achievable and still holds. RISCOF passes the ratified-B suite
  at 29/29 **with the three `Zbc` tests excluded by name and the boundary
  recorded**, plus the Zbkb subset of `K` and both `Zicond` tests. riscv-formal
  passes the union of `rv32im`, `rv32ib` and `rv32iZbkb` at **depth 14
  unchanged**. 1000 random programs containing B, Zbkb and Zicond at high hazard
  density are byte-identical against Spike, and A3's 10⁶-word decoder
  equivalence covers the new encodings with strict reserved fields intact.
  `misa` reads `0x40001100` with the `B`-bit boundary recorded, and `rvntt_isa.yaml` agrees with the hardware.
  Fmax, DMIPS/MHz, CoreMark/MHz and IPC are re-measured on hardware over three
  passes, **the dual software NTT baseline is re-recorded**, and **§10 M3's
  Keccak/NTT breakdown is reported both with and without B.** The predictor's
  payoff is re-reported on the post-Zicond branch stream.

- [ ] **M7.4** — **Core performance: a faster multiply and the Fmax the
  coprocessor can inherit.** *Satisfied by A24, A25, A26, A27 and A28.* The
  Tier-1 timing probe's OOC Fmax is recorded with its DSP count independently
  confirmed, and **the core's Fmax target is stated as a number derived from
  it.** `MUL` is 2 cycles with `rv32um`, RISCOF `M`, `formal_muldiv`,
  riscv-formal and cosim all passing, and `cycle_model.py`'s changed latency term
  still predicting spans exactly. A26's levers are each measured separately with
  **benchmark cycle counts bit-identical throughout** — the proof they were
  timing changes and nothing else — and A27's floorplan configurations likewise,
  validated on hardware because simulation cannot see a floorplan. Fmax is
  re-measured by A12's binary search on post-route WNS with the spread reported
  per A19's non-monotonicity finding, the final critical path is named, and
  **§9's core-only baseline is updated to this number.** The software NTT
  baseline is re-recorded a second time and A23's is kept.

- [ ] **M7.5** — **The cryptographic guarantees: `Zkr` and `Zkt`.**
  *Satisfied by A29 and A30.* The entropy source's repetition-count and
  adaptive-proportion health tests are **shown by fault injection to fire** on
  stuck-at-0, stuck-at-1 and biased sources and to latch `DEAD`; the `seed` state
  machine passes its directed tests; the ML-KEM KATs pass unchanged with the
  entropy path **structurally absent** from that build; and the statement that no
  SP 800-90B validation campaign has been run and the source is **uncertified**
  is in the write-up. `Zkt` is proved for every implemented listed instruction,
  the proof is **observed to fail** under an injected data-dependent early-out,
  the not-implemented portions of the list are reported as not-implemented rather
  than as passing, and **both boundaries — that `Zkt` does not cover control
  flow, and that this core's branch predictor introduces data-dependent timing
  through persistent BTB and counter state — are written down.**

**Why three milestones and not one.** The same reason M7.1 and M7.2 were split:
**a milestone that other work depends on must stay claimable if everything after
it is abandoned.**

- **M7.3 is the one the rest of the project depends on.** B changes the software
  NTT baseline that §10 M2 and M3 are computed against, changes §10 M3's Keccak
  term, and grows §C4's `SchedMachineModel` obligation. If this round stops after
  M7.3, Track B and Track C proceed against a correct and final baseline.
- **M7.4 is performance, and nothing outside it depends on the number.** It is
  also the milestone with the most external uncertainty — A27's floorplanning may
  return zero, and A24's probe may cap the target below 110 MHz. Neither outcome
  blocks anything.
- **M7.5 is two security claims that stand alone.** They depend on the machine
  being finished, which is why they are last, but nothing depends on *them*.
  If the entropy source cannot be made to work, A29's stop rule ships the
  verifiable two-thirds and records the gap, and `Zkt` is unaffected either way.

**No step in this round gets its own milestone**, following `MODS_A`'s reasoning
about A17: eleven steps and three milestones is the right ratio, and giving A24
or A26 a milestone would invite claiming an enabler before the thing it enables.

**What would make this round's plan wrong, and what to do about it.** Three
measurements could invalidate the sequencing above, and each has a named
response rather than an assumption that it will not happen:

1. **A24's probe returns below 110 MHz.** Then the Fmax target is Track B's to
   set, A26 and A27 aim at the lower number, and 110 MHz is reported as *bounded
   by the coprocessor* rather than missed. This is the most likely of the three.
2. **A21 lands B on the critical path anyway.** Then §3.4's central design
   decision failed and the step halts before A22 rather than carrying a
   regression into the Fmax phase.
3. **A26's lever 2 cannot be made correct across the interlock and flush cases.**
   Then it is dropped, A26 reports levers 1, 3 and 4, and the Fmax target falls
   by roughly 1.0–1.5 ns worth — about 10 MHz at this frequency. Lever 1 alone is
   still the largest single contribution in the round.

**This plan is a draft, not a contract.** It will be revised as measurements come
in, and each revision will be stated in the status report at the phase boundary
where it happens, with the measurement that caused it.
